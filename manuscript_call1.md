## Highlights

- **ecodish365** scores what people actually eat — packaged products, homemade meals, and full 24-h recall days — across five complementary indicators (HEFI-2019, HENI, HSR, Food Compass, ReCiPe LCA) anchored to the Canadian Nutrient File, with West African foods via WAFCT 2019.
- AI is confined to tasks databases cannot resolve alone: a **post-hoc normaliser** turns label photos into structured Nutrition Facts (88.2 % field accuracy on a benchmark panel); embedding retrieval links CNF foods to LCA inventories and to USDA Food Patterns equivalents for HENI food-group attribution — all at deterministic runtime cost after one-time build.
- On a **25-day curated panel**, nutrition indicators agree (HEFI–HENI Spearman ρ = 0.84) while diverging modestly from environmental intensity (HENI–GW ρ = −0.59); four literature-anchored diet shifts replicate expected directions, and targeted substitutions move Western processed days from lose–lose toward win–win on health and footprint.
- A **quality-guarded substitution engine** ranks ingredient swaps against whole-day HEFI, HENI, food-pattern gaps, and environmental impact — blocking anatomically implausible matcher noise while surfacing curated reformulation paths such as milk→fortified soy beverage.
- A **reconstruction benchmark for AI recipe decomposition** — the first to score decomposed dishes against their own measured composition — shows that language-model decomposition reproduces food-group shape but overstates the energy of cooked and diluted dishes about two-fold. ## Abstract (~250 words, draft)

Translating what someone ate into actionable nutrition and sustainability feedback requires more than a single score. A granola bar, a homemade stew, and a full day of eating each need different entry paths, yet most tools force users into one. We built **ecodish365**, an open-source decision-support platform that scores real eating patterns across five peer-reviewed indicators — Canada's Food Guide adherence (HEFI-2019), disease-burden minutes (HENI), Health Star Rating, Food Compass, and ReCiPe 2016 life-cycle impact — all from the Canadian Nutrient File, extended with 1,028 West African foods (WAFCT 2019).

Artificial intelligence appears in bounded roles that the literature supports: a hybrid rule-and-LLM categorizer maps foods to Global Burden of Disease risk factors; embedding retrieval links CNF entries to Agribalyse inventories and to USDA Food Patterns Equivalents for HENI's food-group attribution (one-time ~$1 build, zero marginal cost per score); multimodal extraction turns Nutrition Facts photos into structured JSON via a **post-hoc normaliser** that recovered 88.2 % field accuracy where prompt engineering alone failed on dual-column panels; and a recipe decomposer turns free-text dish names into CNF ingredient lists for 24-h recall workflows. Users can describe meals, scan packaged labels, or **pick individual foods by search or AI** — then review, edit, and save days locally before routing to any scorer.

We validate cross-indicator coherence (HENI vs HEFI ρ = 0.89 on a six-meal panel; ρ = 0.84 on 25 curated days), four Stylianou-style diet-shift counterfactuals (4/4 directional checks pass), and a substitution layer with culinary plausibility filters, discovery quality gates, and **FPED-aware ranking** that prioritises swaps closing fruit, vegetable, and whole-grain gaps. On the 25-day panel, better nutrition correlates with lower per-kcal greenhouse-gas intensity (ρ ≈ −0.4 to −0.6), but the relationship is weak enough that multi-metric reporting remains essential. Western processed days improve markedly when targeted substitutions are applied in silico (HEFI 22→53; environmental single-score −87 %). We also stress-test the recipe decomposer against ground truth: a four-lens reconstruction benchmark shows that language-model decomposition preserves food-group shape but overstates the energy of cooked and diluted composites about two-fold, and a catalog-preference policy that defers to a food's own measured composition when the dish is already catalogued removes the error for those foods, lowering the share of composite dishes flagged for nutrient error from 55 % to 0 % in the production pipeline. The platform ships as a reproducible Python/Rust toolchain (Apache 2.0) with auditable prompts, factors, and smoke-test harnesses.

**Keywords:** food systems; diet quality; life-cycle assessment; large language models; Canadian Nutrient File; decision support; 24-hour dietary recall; nutrient profiling.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Why multi-indicator scoring needs bounded AI

Food choices sit at the intersection of health, environment, and culture — yet most digital tools report a single number. Nutrient profiling (HSR, Food Compass), guideline adherence (HEFI-2019), disease-burden minutes (HENI), and life-cycle impact (ReCiPe) each capture a different slice of what "better eating" means, and they do not always move together (Cardinaals et al., 2024). At the same time, translating a real day of eating into scorable data remains painfully manual: producing HEFI-2019 variables from 24-h recalls cost **> 75 hours of registered-dietitian time** in the Canadian Food Intake Screener validation study (Hutchinson et al., 2023), and branded packaged foods rarely appear as discrete entries in national composition tables.

That is where bounded AI helps — not as a black-box scorer, but as a linker. Modern food databases (CNF, FNDDS/FPED, Agribalyse) are heterogeneous in coverage and labelling; LLMs have recently shown near-expert performance on food classification ([Ase et al., 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/1/23)) and retrieval-augmented recipe mapping ([NutriRAG, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11957177/)). The design question for *Nature Food* readers is therefore practical: where can AI reduce friction without replacing the peer-reviewed epidemiology and LCA math that must stay deterministic and auditable?

### 1.2 The gap in current tools

Existing platforms either (a) optimise for a single dimension — diet quality (HEFI-2019, HEI-2015, NRF), nutrient profiling (HSR, Nutri-Score, FCS), carbon footprint, or DALYs — or (b) integrate two dimensions but treat the others as fixed (e.g. nutritional LCA with carbon only). Multi-indicator tools that combine epidemiological DALY-weighted health burden with ReCiPe-style LCA and monetary externalities at the level of individual food items, with explicit uncertainty quantification and open AI augmentation, are rare. The complementarity of nutrient density and disease burden in nutritional LCA was articulated by [Stylianou and colleagues](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2024.1304752/full) but the implementation pipelines and prompts have not been openly released.

Critically, the *authors of the dominant validated nutrient-profiling system themselves* set out the integration this paper performs. O'Hearn et al. (2022, Discussion p. 10) write that "the long-term vision of Food Compass is to score additional features... such as environmental sustainability, social justice, and animal welfare — one for each direction of the compass," yet no openly-released implementation links Food Compass to a peer-reviewed LCIA framework. Likewise the FCS-10 authors (Barrett et al., 2025, Discussion pp. 14–15) state that "use of artificial intelligence large language models could also facilitate the recognition and interpretation of ingredients lists" — an authoritative call for exactly the LLM-based food classification that our pipeline supplies (§3.4).

A second conceptual authority motivates our multi-indicator design. Weidema and Stylianou (2020, *Int J LCA* 25(7):1210–1216) argue that nutrition plays **two distinct roles** in food LCA that prior tools conflated: nutrition as a *function* (best captured by satiety in the functional unit, not by weighted nutrient-profiling scores) and nutrition as an *impact pathway* (the marginal health effect of a food, quantified in DALYs via GBD epidemiology — operationalised as DANI and HENI). They show that loading nutrient-profiling scores into the LCA functional unit can yield conceptually broken results (including negative functional units for limiting nutrients) and recommend separating the two roles. Our platform follows that recommendation literally: HEFI-2019, HSR and FCS-10 are reported as their own diet-quality/profiling indicators alongside HENI (the disease-burden impact pathway) and the ReCiPe 2016 LCA, never collapsed into a single composite score.

Two further methodological gaps compound this. First, the dominant European reference databases — AGRIBALYSE 3.2 and ecoinvent — apply the European Commission's Product Environmental Footprint (PEF) method, which uses 16 indicators with fixed weights, rather than the 18-midpoint / 3-endpoint ReCiPe 2016 family used by most diet-health LCA studies (ADEME, 2024; Huijbregts et al., 2017, p. 141, Table 1). PEF and ReCiPe outputs are **not** numerically interchangeable. Second, no large reference database publishes quantitative uncertainty (e.g. standard deviations) per impact factor: AGRIBALYSE explicitly notes that estimating these would require data not currently available, providing only a qualitative 1–5 Data Quality Rating (DQR), with 67 % of products rated at DQR ≤ 3 (ADEME, 2024, *Méthodologie ACV* page). Any tool aspiring to decision-support credibility therefore has to wrap point-estimate factors in propagated uncertainty bands and cross-database harmonisation logic — which is what we build in §3.8 and §3.5 respectively.

A practical motivation also runs through this paper: producing HEFI-2019 variables manually from 24-hour recalls cost **> 75 hours of registered-dietitian time** in the Canadian Food Intake Screener validation study (Hutchinson et al., 2023, Discussion p. 630). Automated, recipe-level scoring is not an academic luxury — it is the prerequisite for running multi-indicator assessment at population scale.

### 1.3 Contributions

This paper makes the following contributions:

1. **A unified, open-source assessment framework** integrating five complementary indicators on top of the Canadian Nutrient File (§3).
2. **Three AI subsystems with clearly delimited roles** — risk-factor categorization (§3.4), food-to-LCA matching (§3.5), and a FPED-grounded CNF → FNDDS composition bridge that resolves HENI food-group attribution at runtime cost $0 by amortising a one-time ~$1 LLM build over all future scoring calls (§3.6) — each accompanied by a controlled benchmark against expert labels or the source paper's methodology (§4.1–§4.2).
2'. **Cross-indicator internal-consistency demonstration** showing that HENI, HEFI-2019 and HSR independently rank a 6-meal benchmark panel in Spearman rank correlation ρ = 0.886 (HENI vs HEFI) and 0.771 (HEFI vs HSR) after the §3.6 composition fix lands — addressing the "do these indicators actually measure overlapping diet-quality signal at the meal level?" question the multi-indicator framing must answer (§4.5).
3. **Uncertainty quantification** via Monte Carlo propagation and Sobol sensitivity over LCA characterization factors (§3.8, §4.3), addressing a recurring critique of food LCA tools.
4. **A Canadian case study** linking the framework to the 2015 Canadian Community Health Survey–Nutrition and four counterfactual diet shifts (§5).
5. **A "sustainability of AI" audit** of the pipeline's own compute, energy and dollar footprint (§5.5).
6. **A fully reproducible release** (Python/Rust, Apache 2.0) including all factors, prompts, benchmark labels and notebooks.
7. **Multimodal packaged-food ingestion (PKG-IMG-1 + PKG-RECALL-1)** closing the friction gap between branded retail products and the five CNF-FoodID-dependent scorers: multimodal LLM extraction of Nutrition Facts + ingredient lists, a load-bearing post-hoc schema normaliser (§3.8.7.1), HSR-only standalone scanning, and per-occasion label scanning inside the 24-h recall wizard so a user can photograph a granola bar at breakfast and a homemade stew at dinner in one aggregated day (§3.8.7.2).
8. **Ingredient substitution analysis (SUBST-1)** operationalising Stylianou et al. (2021)-style targeted diet shifts as explicit, mass-preserving ingredient counterfactuals with a six-metric scorecard, culinary plausibility guards, discovery quality gates, and FPED-aware gap-fill ranking — validated on four canonical CNF swaps and overlaid onto the S4-lite panel (§3.8.8, §5.2).
9. **Flexible 24-h recall entry** — text description, direct CNF search with AI-assisted lookup, packaged-label scanning, editable daily review, and local recall-history editing — so users can build auditable day-level compositions without manual FoodID enumeration (§3.8 Feature 3).
10. **FPED food-group exposure panel** on the improve-product workflow, surfacing which USDA Food Patterns equivalents a day under- or over-delivers relative to CFG-aligned targets before substitution (§3.8.8).
11. **A reconstruction benchmark and fix for AI recipe decomposition (§3.8.9, §4.7)** — a four-lens, ground-truth-anchored accuracy benchmark (nutrient reconstruction against each food's own measured composition, FPED food-group cosine, structural gates, and agreement with USDA's authoritative FNDDS recipe) that exposed a systematic two-fold calorie overcount on cooked and diluted composite dishes, together with a catalog-preference and nutrient-reconstruction-gate policy plus a cooked-form prompt rule that resolve it.
12. **A food-group exposure layer (FPED) with ingredient-level attribution (FPID) and a cohort view (§3.6)** — USDA Food Pattern equivalents surfaced across the scorecard, the HENI view, dietary-pattern drivers, and the substitution layer; FPID attribution of each food group in a composite to its source ingredients; per-group distribution statistics across saved recall days; and a form-and-state-aware repair of the CNF/WAFCT → FNDDS bridge that removed systematic mismatches of ingredient-form foods to finished products.

**FCS choice.** Our pipeline implements **FCS-10** (Barrett et al., 2025) rather than the full 54-attribute FCS because (a) FCS-10 is fully reproducible — every component is published in the Methods and Supplementary Tables 1–4, whereas the full-FCS *code* is withheld by Tufts pending commercial licensing (Barrett et al., 2024, Data availability; O'Hearn et al., 2022, Data/Code availability); (b) FCS-10 validates against the full FCS at Spearman r = 0.93 (89 % within ±1 unit) with 87 % recommendation-category classification accuracy (Barrett et al., 2025, Results pp. 11–12); (c) FCS-10's inputs match what our recipe-level pipeline actually has — the Nutrition Facts panel plus the ingredient list. We retain the full-FCS framework for diet-level (i.FCS) reporting whenever inputs are complete. Where appropriate, the food-level FCS-10 score is mapped back to the FCS 1–100 scale (≥70 / 31–69 / ≤30 ↔ ≥7 / 4–6 / ≤3) for comparability.

### 2.2 AI for food classification and LCA matching

Recent work establishes the empirical envelope for LLMs on food-classification tasks. Frontier models (Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.1, Gemini 3) reach 90.3–94.2 % expert agreement on binary healthy/unhealthy food classification, with structured (NOVA + WHO) prompts buying recall on the unhealthy class (0.96–0.98) at modest cost in specificity (0.80–0.84) — a recall/specificity trade-off rather than uniform superiority of structured prompting ([Ase et al., 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/1/23)). Retrieval-augmented prompting lifts food-name → 51-class classification from F1 = 0.74 (standard GPT-4) to F1 = 0.82 (RAG GPT-4; +8.4 F1-pt gain; [Zhou et al., 2025 — NutriRAG](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.19.25324268v1)). On Canadian branded packaged foods (n = 33,917 of the FLIP database), sentence-BERT + XGBoost reaches 0.98 accuracy on Health Canada Table of Reference Amounts categorization and R² = 0.98 (MSE 2.5) on FSANZ nutrient-profiling-score prediction from structured nutrient inputs versus R² = 0.84–0.87 from label text — the most pipeline-adjacent published benchmark for our HSR computation ([Hu et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2022.11.022)). Domain fine-tuning of Llama-3 8B (LoRA) raises traffic-light and food-ontology-linking macro F1 from a 5-shot prompting floor of 0.33–0.51 to 0.86–0.97 ([Gjorgjevikj et al., 2026 — FoodyLLM](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crfs.2026.101351)), defining the prompting-only ceiling our §3.4 design accepts in exchange for auditability. For image-based dietary assessment, multimodal LLMs approach but do not yet meet expert nutritional content estimation (~36 % MAPE; [Fridolfsson et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cdnut.2025.107556)).

The closest task-level prior art for food → LCA linkage is twofold. [Furrer et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.143198) semi-automate the EuroFIR ↔ Agribalyse interlinkage via a manually-curated LanguaL™ harmonized-descriptor connection list with 2 / 54 = 3.7 % manual-validation error on single foods; composite foods (22 % of EuroFIR; 2 % of Agribalyse) are *structurally excluded* because recipe-formulation gaps make descriptor matching unreliable. [Krahmer (2024) — LEAF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.climatenlp-1.13/) predicts a food's PEF Environmental Footprint score from its product name via a frozen multilingual sentence-embedding base (135M / 561M parameters) plus a trained closed-set classifier head over 2,518 Agribalyse classes, reaching accuracy 0.73–0.77 and beating GPT-3.5 zero-shot (accuracy 0.37) by ~2× — with GPT-3.5 hallucinating non-existent class labels at 0.19, a finding that directly motivates constraining LLM output to retrieved valid candidates. To our knowledge no published system uses an LLM-assisted *open* retrieval-and-ranking matcher with confidence-scored fallback to link structured Canadian nutrition-database entries — *including composite meals* — to peer-reviewed inventories re-scored under ReCiPe 2016 with multi-country parameterisation and Monte Carlo uncertainty. Our §3.5 contribution therefore rests on four hard distinctions from this prior art: (i) CNF (Canadian) as the source food-composition database rather than EuroFIR (Furrer et al.) or Open Food Facts product names (LEAF), (ii) explicit composite-food / meal-level support, (iii) ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 with workbook-grounded endpoint characterisation factors at all three cultural perspectives (I/H/E) and per-country endpoint CFs for the spatially-explicit categories (loaded from the published RIVM workbooks via a deterministic SHA-256-checksummed ETL; §3.9), and (iv) open retrieve-and-rank with a 0–1 confidence score and logged group-default fallback rather than a closed-set classifier head (LEAF) or a deterministic descriptor-set match (Furrer et al.).

### 2.3 Uncertainty in food LCA

Monte Carlo propagation remains the dominant approach for LCA uncertainty ([Heijungs review; MDPI 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4060/3/3/39); [2D-MC, 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11367-022-02041-0)). Global sensitivity analysis with correlated inputs has been formalised recently ([Kim, 2025, JIE](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.70036)). We adopt log-normal characterization-factor distributions parameterised from Poore & Nemecek's reported between-producer variability.

### 2.4 Sustainability of AI

The pipeline's own footprint matters: training GPT-3 was reported at 1 287 MWh / 552 t CO₂e, and hyperscaler water withdrawals have grown sharply since 2016 ([NTT DATA, 2025](https://www.nttdata.com/global/en/news/press-release/2025/october/102800); [E3S, 2025](https://www.e3s-conferences.org/articles/e3sconf/abs/2025/75/e3sconf_geome2025_03007/e3sconf_geome2025_03007.html)). We therefore audit the per-meal AI inference cost and footprint (§5.5).

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Platform architecture

`ecodish365` is a monorepo with three layers (Figure 1, *to draw*):

- **Frontend**: Next.js 15 (TypeScript), accessible at the page level via [`frontend/src/app/`](frontend/src/app/), exposing per-indicator and per-meal views.
- **Backend**: Django 5 with REST endpoints in [`backend/api/`](backend/api/) and module-specific services in [`backend/{hefi,heni,hsr,fcs,environmental_impact_model,meals}/`](backend/).
- **Numerical core**: a Rust crate (`rust_core`, PyO3 bindings) implementing the HSR, FCS and HENI scoring kernels for deterministic, performance-critical paths (see [`backend/rust_core/`](backend/rust_core/)).

The Canadian Nutrient File (CNF) is the canonical food catalogue. Foods are referenced by `FoodID`; quantities flow through the pipeline as grams or Reference Amounts as appropriate per indicator.

### 3.2 Indicators

All five indicators are implemented exactly per their published definitions (see [`hefi_technical_report.md`](hefi_technical_report.md) and [`heni_technical_report.md`](heni_technical_report.md) for the detailed component-by-component algorithms). For brevity we omit equations here and report only the salient design decisions:

- **HEFI-2019** uses the 10 components and the scoring standards of Brassard et al. (2022a, Table 2 p. 600), with linear interpolation between minimum and maximum standards (Brassard et al., 2022a, Results p. 599). The sodium-density component uses Brassard's published threshold band — max-score at < 0.9 mg/kcal (10 pts), zero-score at ≥ 2.0 mg/kcal (0 pts) — with the ratio computed as `sodium_mg / energy_kcal` in mg/kcal directly. Food classification follows the inclusion/exclusion list of Table A1 of that paper (pp. 606–609) literally — including the choices that fruit juice is *excluded* from V&F, all potatoes (any preparation) count as vegetables, processed meats appear only in the denominator of the Protein component, and regular-fat (3.25 %) milk counts in the Beverages numerator. We retain HEFI-2019's published interpretive guidance: a single one-day score is *not* interpretable as usual adherence (Brassard et al., 2022b, Discussion p. 588), there is no absolute "aligned-diet" threshold (Brassard et al., 2022a, Discussion p. 603), and total and component scores are always reported together (Brassard et al., 2022b, Conclusion p. 589). Two external databases are mandatory: Health Canada's Table of Reference Amounts (2016) and the Rana et al. (2021) free-sugars supplement to CNF 2015 — the CNF itself does not carry a free-sugars field. Until the Rana et al. (2021) supplement is integrated, the C9 free-sugars component uses CNF `SUGARS, TOTAL` as a conservative proxy with an explicit `c9_imputation_note` returned in the API response (per Brassard et al., 2022a Discussion p. 603). The canonical perfect-diet inputs (Vegetables and Fruits ratio = 0.50, all other adequacy ratios at their max thresholds, all moderation ratios at or below their max, sodium-density 0.75 mg/kcal) reproduce **80/80** exactly in our pipeline. Empirical validation on three CNF-constructed 1-day diets (deep-fried fast-food anti-pattern, mixed-balanced lunch+dinner, CFG-2019-aligned plant-forward) — [`backend/_smoke_hefi_canonical_diets.py`](backend/_smoke_hefi_canonical_diets.py), results at [`_smoke_hefi_canonical_diets_results.json`](backend/_smoke_hefi_canonical_diets_results.json) — scores them at **13.6 / 51.5 / 58.8** out of 80 respectively, preserving the expected directional rank (anti-pattern < mixed < ideal) and falling in the Brassard 2022 p10 / p25–p75 / p90 bands. See [`backend/hefi_calculator/hefi/algorithm.py`](backend/hefi_calculator/hefi/algorithm.py).
- **HENI** is the Health Nutritional Index of Stylianou et al. (2021), the operational successor to the CONE-LCA framework of Stylianou et al. (2016). The per-food score is the **net minutes of healthy life gained (+) or lost (−)** from all-cause premature mortality and morbidity per reference serving, attributable to adding a marginal amount of that food to the current diet. The scoring formula (Stylianou et al., 2021, Results p. 617):
$$\text{HENI}_i \;=\; -0.53 \;\times\; \sum_{r=1}^{16} \mathrm{DRF}_r \times d_{i,r}$$
where DRF_r is the cumulative age- and gender-adjusted dietary risk factor (μDALY/g) for risk component *r* and d_{i,r} is the amount of that component in food *i* (g per serving). The **−0.53 min/μDALY constant** is the unit conversion 1 μDALY = 10⁻⁶ × 365 × 24 × 60 ≈ 0.53 min of healthy life, with the negative sign flipping the damage-oriented μDALY into a benefit-oriented minutes-gained metric (Stylianou et al., 2021 Supplementary Information, p. 98, line 1291).
- The sum is over **16 risk components derived from 15 GBD dietary risks** (Stylianou et al., 2021 SI Suppl. Table 3 p. 8), with **fibre split by source** to avoid double-counting: `fibre_other` covers both colorectal cancer and ischaemic heart disease, while `fibre_fvlw` (fibre from fruit, vegetables, legumes, whole grains) covers colorectal cancer only because the IHD benefit is already mediated through the f/v/l/w DRFs (Stylianou et al., 2021 SI §S2.9 pp. 35–36). A **milk-vs-calcium carve-out** is also required: DRF_milk applies *only* to foods classified as milk and flavoured milk; for all other foods the calcium DRF carries the colorectal-cancer benefit alone (Stylianou et al., 2021, Methods p. 626).
- The 15 GBD dietary risk factors and their TMRELs (theoretical-minimum-risk effective intake) are defined in GBD 2017 (GBD 2017 Diet Collaborators, *Lancet* 2019;393:1958–1972, Table p. 1960); the 15-factor scaffold persists unchanged in GBD 2023 (GBD 2023 Collaborators, *Lancet* 2025;406:1873–1922, Table 2 p. 1888), with one diet-relevant TMREL revision (diet high in trans fatty acids; p. 1880). Our pipeline pins the DRF μDALY/g values to the published Stylianou et al. (2021) Suppl. Table 3 (GBD 2016 vintage); a recompute on GBD 2019 RRs follows the Dutch precedent of Cardinaals et al. (2024) and is logged as future work. The canonical worked example for unit-testing — an 85 g chicken-wing serving with 1.85 g PUFA, 0.0281 g calcium, 0.492 g sodium and 0.139 g TFA, which Stylianou et al. (2021 SI §S2.2 p. 13) report at **−3.3 min/serving** — reproduces in our pipeline at **−3.257 min/serving** (Δ = +0.04 min) when the synthetic risk-factor vector is injected directly into the DALY kernel (15 of 15 unit tests pass).

Two complementary harnesses validate the live API path with CNF substrate. The **CNF-native implementation regression** ([`backend/_smoke_heni_literature_panel.py`](backend/_smoke_heni_literature_panel.py); results at [`_smoke_heni_literature_panel_results.json`](backend/_smoke_heni_literature_panel_results.json)) computes the expected HENI for each panel food directly from CNF's per-100g nutrient values × Stylianou DRFs + TMRELs (mirroring the Rust kernel arithmetic) and asserts that the API output matches within ±0.1 min. The harness reports **10/10 PASS** across the 10-food panel spanning the dynamic range (chicken wing 629 +0.10 min; hot dog 4644 −6.7; pizza 4962 −5.0; sardines 3054 −1.7; corned beef 2791 −10.4; broccoli 2026 +4.9; whole milk 113 +0.4). - **HSR** is implemented per the Australian/NZ **HSR System Implementation Guide v9** (HSRAC, 10 December 2025), Appendix 1 Tables 1–7. The **NOVA classification layer** (one of FCS-10's 18 attributes, contributing to the Food Ingredients domain) was rebuilt 2026-05-23 from a substring-keyword block into a rigorous Monteiro-2019-grounded classifier at [`backend/fcs_calculator/fcs/utils/nova_classifier.py`](backend/fcs_calculator/fcs/utils/nova_classifier.py). The Hierarchist 100-yr global-warming potentials we encode are CO₂ = 1, CH₄ (biogenic) = 34, CH₄ (fossil) = 36, N₂O = 298 (RIVM 2017, pp. 29–34, Table 2.2), with the constant midpoint-to-endpoint factor structure of Eq. (1) on p. 139 of Huijbregts et al. 2017 (fossil resource scarcity excepted; RIVM 2017, p. 25, Table 1.5, footnote 3). We use the global default characterization factors and report country-specific results from the chapter S1–S7 supplements where Canadian values are available (water use, fine PM, photochemical ozone, terrestrial acidification, freshwater eutrophication). See [`backend/environmental_impact_model/src/life_cycle_assessment.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/life_cycle_assessment.py).

**Choice of method: ReCiPe vs PEF.** Our pipeline uses ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 Hierarchist rather than the European Commission's PEF method (the LCIA framework underlying AGRIBALYSE 3.2; ADEME, 2024). This choice is principled but consequential: PEF and ReCiPe diverge in (i) category list (PEF has 16 indicators, ReCiPe 18 midpoint + 3 endpoint), (ii) eutrophication treatment (PEF splits into terrestrial / marine / freshwater; ReCiPe keeps freshwater and marine), (iii) weighting (PEF imposes a single fixed weight set; ReCiPe offers I/H/E cultural perspectives), and (iv) value-choice operationalisation (PEF embeds one set; ReCiPe makes the choice transparent). We adopt ReCiPe because the diet-health LCA literature is built on it (Stylianou et al., 2016, 2021; Dekker et al., 2019; Heller et al., 2013), because the Hierarchist perspective aligns with WHO and intergovernmental conventions (Huijbregts et al., 2017, p. 140), and because the published v1.1 update preserves food-product rankings against ReCiPe 2008 with Spearman ρ between 0.85 and 0.99 across all impact categories and perspectives (Dekker et al., 2019, §3.1, p. 3) — making the method choice low-risk for ranking-based conclusions even where absolute magnitudes change. We use AGRIBALYSE 3.2 only as a source of LCI data (not LCIA), re-applying ReCiPe characterisation factors to the underlying inventories where ecoinvent licensing permits, and treat any direct PEF-vs-ReCiPe comparison as a sensitivity analysis rather than a primary result. **Concretely:** our §3.5 matcher ingests the published `AGRIBALYSE3.2_Tableur produits alimentaires_PublieAOUT25.xlsx` Synthese tab (ADEME, 2024 — 2,425 Ciqual-keyed food products, deterministic SHA-256–pinned ETL artefact at [`backend/environmental_impact_model/data/agribalyse_v32_catalog.json`](backend/environmental_impact_model/data/agribalyse_v32_catalog.json)) into a *dual-namespace catalogue* — each row carries both a ReCiPe-equivalent subset (climate change, three published climate sub-components, stratospheric ozone — the only EF 3.1 indicators that map cleanly to ReCiPe 2016 H midpoints) and the full 16-indicator EF 3.1 payload with native units (`mol H⁺ eq/kg`, `disease inc./kg`, `CTUh/kg`, `CTUe/kg`, `Pt/kg`, `m³ depriv./kg`, `kg Sb eq/kg`, …). The pipeline aggregation consumes the climate-change total and the stratospheric-ozone-depletion midpoint per matched row; the three EF climate sub-components (fossil, biogenic, land-use change) are carried in the catalogue and surfaced via the per-meal `recipe2016_h_ef31_sensitivity` audit block for §4.4 / §5 supplementary inspection, but are not summed into the aggregated midpoint vector, endpoint, or single score, since the EF climate total already equals their sum (identity holds within 1 % across all 2,425 v32 rows) and ReCiPe 2016 defines a single global-warming midpoint. Fourteen EF categories — ionising radiation, photochemical ozone formation, particulate matter, human toxicity (cancer and non-cancer), freshwater ecotoxicity, acidification, eutrophication (freshwater, marine and terrestrial), land use, water use, and resource use (fossils and minerals / metals) — have no clean ReCiPe 2016 equivalent and fall back to the existing `cnf_integrator` group-default ReCiPe values in the ReCiPe-side pipeline. On the ReCiPe side this gap appears as sixteen group-default midpoint categories rather than fourteen, because ReCiPe splits the EF photochemical-ozone indicator into two ozone-formation midpoints (human-health and terrestrial-ecosystem) and the EF freshwater-ecotoxicity indicator into three ecotoxicity midpoints (terrestrial, freshwater and marine); the two counts describe the same gap from the two sides and should not be harmonised. This design makes the EF-vs-ReCiPe divergence explicit per-row rather than silently coercing units across methods.

### 3.4 AI subsystem 1 — Hybrid rule + LLM categorization to GBD risk factors

The HENI risk-factor categorizer ([`backend/heni_calculator/heni/categorization/`](backend/heni_calculator/heni/categorization/)) is a two-stage hybrid:

1. **Rule-based first**: a deterministic categorizer maps CNF food-group, NutrientName, and description tokens to risk-factor scores ∈ [0, 1] for each of 14 GBD risk factors.
2. **LLM augmentation only when needed**: an LLM (default `gpt-4o-mini`, temperature 0, max 150 tokens) is invoked *only* for foods whose rule output is incomplete or low-confidence (any factor with rule-confidence < 0.3). The prompt is restricted to ≤ 5 factors at a time, includes the rule output as context, and requests JSON.

This design constrains LLM usage to the long-tail of ambiguous foods, keeping the pipeline cheap and auditable. The choice is endorsed by the FCS-10 authors themselves, who state that "use of artificial intelligence large language models could also facilitate the recognition and interpretation of ingredients lists" (Barrett et al., 2025, Discussion pp. 14–15), and consistent with the contemporaneous evidence that retrieval-augmented LLMs reach F1 ≈ 0.82 on food identification (Zhou et al., 2025 — NutriRAG) and 90.3–94.2 % expert agreement on structured-prompt food classification (Ase et al., 2026, *Nutrients*).

We use prompting rather than fine-tuning (cf. Gjorgjevikj et al., 2026 — FoodyLLM, which shows that LoRA fine-tuning of Llama-3 8B lifts food→ontology-linking macro F1 from a 5-shot prompting floor of 0.33–0.51 to 0.86–0.97) for four principled reasons: auditability (frozen-weight models with prompt-version control), portability across model vintages, zero training-data cost, and confining LLM influence to the long-tail of ambiguous foods where the rule layer abstains. The FoodyLLM authors themselves recommend retrieval-augmented prompting as the preferred mitigation for the food-ontology-linking brittleness of general-purpose LLMs (Gjorgjevikj et al., 2026, Discussion p. 9), which is the architecture we adopt in §3.5. Our pipeline runs at temperature 0 for determinism — distinct from Ase et al.'s temperature 1.0 baseline, so their published numbers are not a like-for-like benchmark.

### 3.5 AI subsystem 2 — LLM-assisted food-to-LCA matching *(new capability for this paper)*

A central reviewer concern with food LCA tools is hardcoded per-food-group impact factors. We therefore add an LLM-assisted matcher that, for each CNF food, returns its best-matching entry in Agribalyse 3.2 (and falls back to Poore & Nemecek 2018 group means). The candidate catalogue is the deterministically-generated 2,425-entry ETL artefact described in §3.2 (sourced from the Synthese tab of the ADEME *Tableur produits alimentaires* published August 2025; the catalogue replaces an earlier 54-entry hand-curated bootstrap used during development). The matcher uses retrieval-augmented prompting — structurally a NutriRAG-style retrieve-then-rank pipeline (Zhou et al., 2025): candidate Agribalyse entries are first retrieved by cosine similarity over OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` 1536-dim sentence embeddings of food descriptions (top-k = 20, the upper end of the k = 5–20 sweep Zhou et al. found optimal), then ranked by an LLM (production default `gpt-4.1-mini`, with multi-provider routing to Anthropic `claude-haiku-4-5` via the `ChatJSONClient` abstraction in [`backend/environmental_impact_model/src/llm_client.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/llm_client.py); temperature 0, JSON-constrained output) with explicit reasoning over food composition, processing and provenance. Following Krahmer's (2024) LEAF benchmark, which observed GPT-3.5 hallucinating non-existent Agribalyse class labels at 0.19, the LLM is constrained to rank only from the retrieved candidate set and is never permitted to free-generate an ID; any LLM response whose `ciqual_code` is not in the retrieved set is rejected at validation time and falls back to the group default. Outputs include the matched Ciqual code, a 0–1 confidence score, and a short justification. Confidence below a threshold (default 0.6) triggers fallback to the food-group default, with the decision logged for auditing. The matcher sits *upstream* of the existing `cnf_integrator.get_environmental_impact_factors` group-default path as a feature-flagged override (`enable_lca_matcher`, default off), so existing API consumers see no behavioural change unless they explicitly opt in. See [`backend/environmental_impact_model/src/lca_matcher.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/lca_matcher.py). Matcher-overlaid midpoints flow through the workbook-grounded endpoint conversion factor stack described in §3.9 (loaded from the published RIVM ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 workbook via a SHA-256-checksummed ETL; this replaces an earlier hand-typed endpoint factor table whose `terrestrial_ecotoxicity_ecosystem` and `human_toxicity_non_cancer` entries had drifted by 4,737× and 34× respectively from the canonical workbook values — latent drift under the v1 trim but corrected in the same revision).

**Tier β — retrieval-quality improvements (offline).** Two refinements to the retrieval pipeline land in the same code path: (i) a food-state canonicalisation pre-filter `_canonicalize_food_state` strips well-known CNF state modifiers (raw / cooked / boiled / frozen / canned / dried / homemade / partly skimmed / ...) from the description before retrieval, and surfaces them as a `[state: ...]` annotation appended to the LLM ranking prompt; (ii) a subgroup-routing pre-filter `_agribalyse_subgroup_for_cnf` constrains retrieval to the Agribalyse top-level group most likely to contain the right candidates for composite-y CNF groups (Babyfoods → `aliments infantiles`; Soups / Mixed Dishes / Fast Foods → `entrées et plats composés`; Beverages → `boissons`; etc.). Subgroup routing falls back to the full catalogue when the filtered pool would be below the top-k floor, so the LLM ranker is never starved. Both refinements are unit-tested with hand-curated mappings (`test_matcher_canonicalisation.py`); a Jaccard token-overlap regression bound (`_smoke_matcher_coverage.py --before-after`) catches catastrophic regressions in the text path without requiring an OpenAI key, with the caveat that Jaccard cannot measure semantic-retrieval improvements (the production matcher uses OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small`).

**Tier γ — composite-food recipe decomposition (`enable_recipe_decomposer`, on by default in the web UI; opt-in for direct API consumers).** A second LLM-assisted fallback fires under `RecipeDecomposer.should_decompose` when the CNF food's FoodGroupName is in a composite set (`Mixed Dishes`, `Soups, Sauces and Gravies`, `Fast Foods`, `Babyfoods`, `Sausages and Luncheon meats`, `Sweets`, `Snacks`, `Baked Products`) AND the direct matcher either failed (`matched=False`) OR succeeded at confidence below the **`HIGH_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD = 0.85`**. The 0.85 threshold is empirically grounded by the §4.4 matcher accuracy benchmark: in the `0.60–0.85` borderline confidence band the flagged-by-automated-heuristics rate is 43 % vs. 26 % in the `≥ 0.85` band, and the live-LLM smoke evidence shows the matcher returning `matched=True` on stretched LCA-distant near-misses for Canadian composites (Bannock → "Biscuit, extruded and grilled, fruits filling" at 0.65; Tourtière → "Riesling wine and pork pie" at 0.60). Under the old `matched=False`-only trigger the decomposer never activated in practice; under the refined trigger, borderline-confidence matches on composite groups route to ingredient-level reconstruction instead. The new [`RecipeDecomposer`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/recipe_decomposer.py) asks the configured LLM (production default `gpt-4.1-mini`; alternate `claude-haiku-4-5` via `LLM_PROVIDER=anthropic`) to express the dish as a mass-weighted ingredient list with ingredients drawn EXCLUSIVELY from a 30-candidate retrieval pool over the v32 catalogue (constrained-vocabulary output; hallucinated Ciqual codes are rejected at parse time). The decomposition is validated against four gates: (1) **mass conservation** — Σ ingredient masses + unresolved mass = target serving ± `max(5 g, 2 % of target)` (scale-aware: 250 g target → ±5 g, 500 g → ±10 g, 1 kg → ±20 g; admits typical recipe-rounding without admitting wildly imbalanced decompositions). When the LLM's ingredient sum falls short of the target by a non-trivial amount but its unresolved-mass field is 0 (an arithmetic-sloppiness pattern observed live), the shortfall is auto-credited to `unresolved_mass_g` up to the 10 %-of-target cap rather than rejecting; (2) unresolved fraction ≤ 10 % of target; (3) self-reported `decomposition_confidence` ≥ **`0.30`** — an empirically-calibrated floor: live-LLM probing across 8 composites spanning trivial (lasagna, scrambled eggs) to genuinely-hard (poutine, bannock, tourtière) showed `gpt-4o-mini` reports `0.40` confidence for 7 / 8 cases regardless of decomposition difficulty (a model default bias, not true uncertainty calibration). The prior 0.60 threshold was therefore unreachable in practice — Tier γ never resolved any decomposition under that gate. The 0.30 floor admits the empirically-anchored 0.40 bulk while still rejecting "I have no idea" (conf = 0.00) responses; (4) **minimum 2 ingredients** — a single-row "decomposition" is the matcher's job, not the decomposer's; (5) every Ciqual code appears in the retrieved candidate set. The architectural intent is that the structural gates (1, 2, 4, 5) carry the QA load and `decomposition_confidence` is a soft secondary check. Pass-through decompositions resolve each ingredient through the existing v32 catalogue's per-100 g ReCiPe midpoints, multiply by mass / 100 g, and sum mass-weighted to a single per-food impact dict — bypassing the group-mean fallback entirely and **overwriting any borderline matcher match**. The audit trail is surfaced under `recipe_decomposition_decisions[]` (with a `triggered_by` field recording `matcher_failed` or `low_matcher_confidence:<conf>`) in parallel to `lca_matcher_decisions[]`. Cost: ~$0.0003 / composite at temperature 0 (similar order to the matcher's $0.0002 / food); cached per food_id.

**Live evidence (2026-05-22).** Running the refined decomposer against 7 Canadian-specific composite foods (Bannock, Tourtière, Poutine, Butter tart, Shepherd's pie with corn, and two composite babyfoods) under the updated thresholds yields **7 / 7 resolved** decompositions, versus **0 / 7** under the original 0.60 confidence gate. The mass-weighted aggregate Global warming impact moves materially in 5 of 7 cases — most decompositions LOWER the headline GW because the matcher's borderline choice had stretched to a higher-impact LCI entry (Tourtière matched to "Riesling wine and pork pie", an entry the decomposition correctly replaces with short-crust pastry + country-style pâté + leek pie components; Poutine matched to "Mashed potatoes with fresh tome cheese", decomposed correctly into French fries + potato + cheese components). One decomposition (babyfood, beef + vegetables) raises the headline GW substantially (0.36 → 1.78 kg CO₂-eq / 100 kcal) because the matcher's coarse "Vegetable dish for baby with meat/fish and starch" entry under-weighted the real meat content (~30 g of cooked beef stewing meat), which the decomposition correctly attributes ingredient-by-ingredient. This is exactly the within-group-variance problem TODO-CODE-LCA-3 was designed to surface — the decomposition produces a more granular, defensible estimate than any single-row Agribalyse v32 match can express for these dishes.

**Model swap + prompt rewrite (2026-05-22, same revision).** The empirical 0.40 confidence anchor on `gpt-4o-mini` documented above motivated two concurrent changes. (a) **Model upgrade.** The production ranking + decomposition model is now `gpt-4.1-mini` (OpenAI, $0.40 / $1.60 per 1M input/output tokens; matches `gpt-4o` on IFEval at ~83 % lower cost than `gpt-4o`). (b) **Prompt rewrite per Tian et al. (2023, "Just Ask for Calibration", arXiv:2305.14975).** Tian et al. find that asking an RLHF-tuned model for "the probability that it is correct" rather than a generic "how sure are you" reduces expected calibration error by ~50 % across ChatGPT, GPT-4 and Claude. We rewrote both the matcher and decomposer system prompts to (i) frame the confidence field as P(an LCA expert would call this match / decomposition correct), (ii) give discrete numeric anchors (0.95 near-identical → 0.00 no acceptable candidate for the matcher; 0.90 canonical recipe → 0.10 reaching for any ingredient for the decomposer), and (iii) add Lin et al. (2022)-style indirect elicitation to the decomposer ("if you ran this 10 times, what fraction would still be LCA-equivalent?"), since discrete fractional anchors are easier targets than raw floats. **Calibration probe (8 composites, trivial → hard).** On `gpt-4.1-mini` under the rewritten prompts the probe reports 5 distinct confidence values across the panel, std-dev 0.371, and Spearman ρ = −0.417 between hand-labelled difficulty and reported confidence (harder → lower confidence, the correct direction). On the same panel `gpt-4o-mini` produced 1 distinct value (0.40 on 7/8 probes; std-dev ≈ 0). Per-probe artefacts ship at [`backend/environmental_impact_model/data/confidence_probe_<provider>_<model>_<utc>.json`](backend/environmental_impact_model/data/) for reproducibility.

**Multi-provider abstraction (`LLM_PROVIDER` env, default `openai`).** Both the matcher and the decomposer now consume a `ChatJSONClient` Protocol (see [`backend/environmental_impact_model/src/llm_client.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/llm_client.py)); setting `LLM_PROVIDER=anthropic` routes the ranking + decomposition calls to `claude-haiku-4-5` ($1 / $5 per 1M tokens) via Anthropic's assistant-prefill trick (the recommended Anthropic pattern for JSON output, per the Anthropic prompting guide). Embeddings remain OpenAI-only (`text-embedding-3-small`) because Anthropic does not expose an embedding API as of 2026-05. On the same 8-composite probe `claude-haiku-4-5` returns 4 distinct confidence values (std 0.298) but with positive Spearman ρ — Claude tends to collapse trivial composites to a single-ingredient response (which our `min_ingredients = 2` gate rejects); production default therefore stays `openai/gpt-4.1-mini`, with Claude available as an alternative for reproducibility checks and provider-bias robustness arguments. This addresses a recurring reviewer ask that no single vendor's idiosyncrasies dominate downstream results.

Architecturally this is the operationalisation of TODO-CODE-LCA-3's "Tier 2 — recipe decomposition for composites" tier and addresses the dominant residual gap on the long tail of CNF composite foods that no single Agribalyse v32 entry can mirror.

### 3.6 AI subsystem 3 — FPED composition bridge for HENI food-group attribution *(new capability for this paper)*

HENI's published formula `HENI = −0.53 × Σ DRF_r × d_{i,r}` (Stylianou et al., 2021 Results p. 617) requires `d_{i,r}` to be the **grams of risk-component category** *r* in food *i* — fruits, vegetables, milk, red meat, processed meat, nuts/seeds, whole grains, legumes, and sugar-sweetened beverages — not the food's bulk mass. Stylianou et al. (2021) extract these per-food masses from the USDA Food Patterns Equivalents Database (FPED), which is itself derived from the USDA Food and Nutrient Database for Dietary Studies (FNDDS). The CNF does not publish equivalent composition fractions; for the first revision of `ecodish365` we attributed the full 100 g of a CNF food's mass to its single dominant CNF FoodGroup risk factor (e.g. a 150 g pepperoni pizza counted as 150 g of `processed_meat`), TMREL-capped downstream but architecturally incorrect for composite foods. This section describes the FPED-grounded composition lookup that closes the gap.

**Architecture.** A deterministic two-stage ETL produces a per-CNF-FoodID composition table at [`backend/heni_calculator/data/cnf_heni_composition.json`](backend/heni_calculator/data/cnf_heni_composition.json):

1. **CNF → FNDDS bridge** ([`build_cnf_to_fndds_bridge.py`](backend/heni_calculator/heni/etl/build_cnf_to_fndds_bridge.py)). For each CNF FoodID, embed the bilingual description (`FoodDescription` English + `FoodDescriptionF` French — the bilingual signal disambiguates Canadian-specific foods like *bannock* and *tourtière*) with `text-embedding-3-small` and retrieve the top-20 nearest FNDDS 2017-2018 survey foods by cosine similarity; an LLM ranker (`gpt-4.1-mini`, temperature 0, JSON-constrained output) picks the best analog and reports a confidence score. This is **structurally the same retrieve-then-rank pattern as §3.5's LCA matcher** (Zhou et al., 2025 NutriRAG; constrained-vocabulary defence per Krahmer 2024 LEAF — hallucinated `fdc_id` values are rejected at parse time). Confidence ≥ 0.5 is required to accept a bridge; below this the CNF food is marked `unbridged` and runtime HENI scoring falls back to the legacy literal-100 attribution with an explicit audit tag. The bridge is built once, cached on disk (`cnf_to_fndds_bridge.json`), and re-used by every HENI call — runtime cost is a Python dict lookup, not an LLM call.
2. **FNDDS → FPED composition** ([`build_cnf_heni_composition.py`](backend/heni_calculator/heni/etl/build_cnf_heni_composition.py)). Deterministic CSV-join chain: bridged CNF FoodID → FNDDS `food_code` → FPED_1718.xls row → per-column cup-/oz-equivalent values → grams per HENI risk-factor category. Cup-/oz-equivalent → gram conversion is per-column (e.g. milk 244 g/cup-eq, cheese 42.5 g/cup-eq, fruits 154 g/cup-eq, vegetables 165 g/cup-eq, oz-equivalent grains/meat/nuts 28.35 g; USDA MyPlate "What counts as a cup-equivalent?" reference). The FPED-column → HENI risk-factor mapping follows Stylianou's definitions (vegetables = V_TOTAL − V_LEGUMES to avoid double-count with the `legumes` factor; milk = D_MILK + D_YOGURT + D_CHEESE summed at the per-source gram conversions).

**Cost economics.** One-time ETL cost: ~$0.50 for FNDDS embedding + ~$0.50 for the ~5,691 LLM ranking calls ≈ **$1 total**, cached forever in the committed JSON. **Per-HENI-call runtime cost: $0** (Python dict lookup). This dominates the alternative architecture of per-call LLM-mediated decomposition (which would amortise to ~$0.0001 × *N* foods × *M* future scoring calls — a recurring expense that scales with usage).

**Methodological parity with Stylianou.** Because Stylianou et al. (2021) computed HENI on 5,853 WWEIA foods using FPED for the composition layer, our pipeline now reads from the same authoritative source they used. The remaining CNF↔USDA divergence collapses to two narrow components: (i) the CNF↔FNDDS food-selection step (gated by the bridge LLM at ≥ 0.5 confidence; 23 / 23 smoke foods bridged with median confidence 0.90), and (ii) per-100g CNF↔USDA nutrient-table differences (e.g. CNF chicken-wing sodium 98 mg / 100g vs USDA reference 579 mg / 100g — a real Canadian-vs-American food-cataloguing difference, not a methodological artefact).

**Empirical impact on composite foods.** Per-100g HENI for *pepperoni pizza* (CNF 4962): previously attributed 100 g to `processed_meat` (literal-100 attribution); now correctly decomposes to **2.27 g processed_meat + 16.5 g vegetables + 23.8 g milk** (the cheese topping) — an order-of-magnitude correction to the dominant risk-factor mass. Per-100g HENI for *apple pie commercial* (CNF 3941): previously 0 g `fruits` (the CNF FoodGroup "Baked Products" did not trigger the fruits branch of the legacy attribution); now 53.9 g `fruits` (the FPED apple-pie-filling row reports 0.5 cup-eq fruit per 100 g). Pure-category foods (broccoli, whole milk, chicken wing) are unchanged — the legacy attribution was already approximately correct for them, and FPED simply confirms.

**Validation.** The CNF-native implementation regression harness (§3.2, §4.1) imports `classify_ingredient` and `get_composition_for_food` from the same modules the API uses, so the harness's "expected" and the API's "actual" diverge only if there is a real computation bug rather than a copy-paste drift between harness and code. The harness reports 10/10 PASS at the ±0.1 min gate after Phase 4 of the 2026-05-23 implementation. The downstream cross-system Spearman rank correlation between HENI and HEFI moved from ρ = 0.77 to **ρ = 0.886** when the bridge landed (§4.5) — HENI now ranks meals in stronger coherence with HEFI than HEFI does with HSR.

**Coverage.** The bridge now spans the full CNF corpus rather than a smoke-test subset: of 5,993 CNF foods, **5,884 (98.2 %) bridge to an FNDDS analog and 5,576 (93.0 %) resolve to an FPED-grounded composition** (median bridge confidence 0.75). The remaining ~7 % either have a matched FNDDS food_code with no corresponding FPED row (e.g. frostings, glazes) or bridge below the 0.5 confidence threshold, and fall back to the legacy literal-100 attribution, so coverage is never silently broken — only architecturally weaker on those foods. The full bridge is a one-time resumable ETL (≈ $1.2 in LLM tokens, idempotent); it was rebuilt against the CNF 2026 edition and re-runs only over newly-added food codes on any future edition refresh. The complementary USDA Food Patterns *Ingredient* Database (FPID) is now consumed at runtime to attribute each food group in a composite to its source ingredients, as described under the food-group exposure layer below. See [`backend/heni_calculator/heni/etl/`](backend/heni_calculator/heni/etl/) and [`backend/heni_calculator/heni/data/composition_loader.py`](backend/heni_calculator/heni/data/composition_loader.py).

**Bridge integrity: matching food form, not just food category *(2026-05-28)*.** A later audit of the bridge exposed a systematic weakness at its acceptance floor. Of roughly 8,000 accepted bridges, about one in eight sat at exactly the 0.50 confidence threshold, and many of these were *ingredient-form* foods that have no as-consumed analog in FNDDS — raw flours, dry uncooked grains, pure culinary oils and rendered fats — which the ranker had been forced to match to finished products: flour to bread, dry oats to a sweetened ready-to-eat cereal, even seal oil to whole salmon. Because FNDDS catalogues foods as eaten, those matches injected food groups the source food does not contain. We re-ranked the floor-confidence foods with a prompt that judges *form and state* explicitly, so that a genuine form mismatch (raw versus cooked, flour versus bread, oil versus a whole food) is now left unbridged, whereas a legitimate species substitution within the same form (one cooked vegetable or legume standing in for another) is retained. The re-run left the 7,000-plus higher-confidence bridges untouched, correctly unbridged 321 ingredient-form foods, and upgraded 207 others to better analogs. Because it preserves same-form substitutions, it also protected the cross-cultural matches that the West African foods depend on, which is exactly where blanket exclusion would have done the most damage. The repair is deterministic and re-runs only over the affected subset.

**From a HENI input to a first-class food-group exposure layer *(2026-05-28)*.** The same FPED profiles that feed HENI now also drive a food-group exposure layer aimed at researchers and clinicians. For any meal, day, or saved recall, the platform reports the USDA Food Pattern equivalents a diet delivers — cups of fruit and vegetables, ounce-equivalents of whole and refined grains, the protein subgroups, dairy, and added sugars — against MyPlate and an approximation of Canada's Food Guide targets, in plain language for individuals and as a full component table for researchers. This layer is surfaced on the multi-indicator scorecard, inside the HENI analysis view, as the food-group drivers that explain a day's resemblance to a named dietary pattern (§3.8.6), and as the currency in which substitution suggestions are expressed (§3.8.8). A cohort view aggregates the same per-recall profiles across a set of saved days into per-group medians, interquartile ranges, and the share of days that meet each target, which lets the food-group read scale from one day to a population sample without leaving the food-group language clinicians counsel in. Finally, the FPID database answers a question FPED cannot: for a composite food it attributes each food group to the specific ingredients that supply it — the red meat in a lasagna to its ground beef, the dairy to its cheese — drawn from USDA's authoritative recipe rather than from a language model. The same machinery doubles as a reconstruction check on how faithfully the bridged recipe reproduces the food's own food-group profile, which is the deterministic counterpart to the nutrient-reconstruction benchmark of §3.8.9.

### 3.7 Audience-aware API + frontend contract for nutrition indicators *(new capability for this paper)*

A platform that serves both lay end-users (consumer decision support) AND researchers / policymakers (transparency, citation, reproducibility) cannot use a single result presentation. Numerical breakdowns that researchers need for citation — μDALY values, raw HSR baseline/modifying point tiers, FCS pre-rescaling original scores, FPED cup-equivalents, NOVA classifier rationale strings — confuse consumers; literature-grounded prose that consumers need (recommendation bands, mandatory caveats) underserves researchers. We adopt the **`user_type` audience-aware contract** that the platform's environmental endpoint ([`backend/api/views/environmental_views.py`](backend/api/views/environmental_views.py)) had already implemented, extending it to the four nutrition endpoints (HENI / HEFI / HSR / FCS) with literature-cited explanation packs per audience.

**Architecture.** Each of the four endpoints accepts a `user_type ∈ {individual, researcher, policy}` request parameter (default `individual`). After the underlying score is computed, the API attaches a top-level `explanations` block whose shape varies by audience: `individual` mode returns `score_summary` (headline + units + interpretation + **mandatory caveat**) and `action_tips` — no methodology and no math; `researcher` mode adds `methodology` (per-section provenance with citation) and `citations` (formal references); `policy` mode adds `policy_context` (use cases + population-level distributions). The numerical computational state remains in the response for all audiences (researchers can still inspect μDALY values, raw points, original_score, etc.); the frontend decides what to RENDER. This preserves the existing API contract — no breaking changes — while adding the audience-aware presentation layer.

**Literature-grounded explanation packs.** Four new modules carry the audience-specific copy with file:line citations back to the source papers: [`heni_explanations.py`](backend/api/views/heni_explanations.py), [`hefi_explanations.py`](backend/api/views/hefi_explanations.py), [`hsr_explanations.py`](backend/api/views/hsr_explanations.py), [`fcs_explanations.py`](backend/api/views/fcs_explanations.py). For each system, the **mandatory caveat** is enforced in every audience mode and is sourced from the source paper's own scope-limit guidance: HENI's marginality caveat ("not applicable to substantial diet changes", Stylianou 2021 Discussion p. 622); HEFI's single-day caveat ("a single individual HEFI-2019 from one 24-h recall does NOT reflect usual adherence", Brassard 2022b Discussion p. 588); HSR's within-category-only comparison rule (Shahid 2020 p. 1533; HSRAC v9 Introduction); FCS's per-100-kcal cross-category warning (Mozaffarian 2021 Methods + O'Hearn 2022 i.FCS diet-level validation guidance). The previous hardcoded `hefi_interpretation` block and the missing FCS `recommendation` field — both surfaced by an end-to-end audit — were replaced and added respectively by the literature-cited packs.

**Frontend dual-mode rendering.** A reusable [`AudienceToggle`](frontend/src/components/shared/AudienceToggle.tsx) component (extracted from the environmental food-comparison page where the convention originated) and a reusable [`ExplanationsPanel`](frontend/src/components/shared/ExplanationsPanel.tsx) component drop into each of the four nutrition result pages. The panel renders the `score_summary` headline + interpretation + a callout-styled mandatory caveat in all modes; methodology and citations sections appear (collapsible, default-open) in researcher mode; `policy_context` appears in policy mode; the existing math-leaking detailed-breakdown components are conditionally hidden in individual mode via single-line `{userType !== 'individual' && (...)}` wrappers. The audience selection is persisted per-page in component state (default `individual`).

**End-to-end validation.** A contract-test harness ([`backend/_smoke_audience_aware_contract.py`](backend/_smoke_audience_aware_contract.py); results at [`_smoke_audience_aware_contract_results.json`](backend/_smoke_audience_aware_contract_results.json)) makes 52 assertions (4 endpoints × 3 audiences × per-audience checks) covering: (a) the `explanations` block is present in the expected response path; (b) `score_summary` contains a non-empty headline; (c) the mandatory per-audience caveat is present and matches a literature-grounded canonical phrase ("marginal" / "one serving" for HENI; "single-day" / "one day" / "usual adherence" for HEFI; "within" + category for HSR; "per 100" / "cross" for FCS); (d) **individual mode does not leak any of 11 forbidden math tokens** (μDALY, 0.5256, DRF, baseline/modifying, original_score, cup-eq, etc. — substring case-insensitive); (e) researcher mode carries the required literature citations (Stylianou 2021, Brassard 2022, HSRAC v9 / Shahid 2020, Mozaffarian 2021, O'Hearn 2022); (f) policy mode carries the `policy_context` block. **52 / 52 PASS** as of the 2026-05-23 shipping. The five existing nutrition smoke harnesses remain unchanged (default `user_type=individual` matches their existing assumptions); the audience contract is layered on top, never breaking the underlying score computation.

### 3.8 AI subsystem 4 — User-facing food matching and recipe decomposition *(new capability for this paper)*

The four nutrition indicators above all require the user to supply foods either by CNF FoodID directly or via a search box. Until this round, all seven user-facing search surfaces on the platform (CNF Explorer, the four calculate pages, FCS Compare, FCS Food Profile) called a pure-fuzzywuzzy search ([`backend/api/food_id_finder.py:176-242`](backend/api/food_id_finder.py#L176-L242)). That implementation is reliable for exact CNF entries ("apple raw", "white bread") but fails at three places researchers and consumers both hit: (a) **synonyms and foreign-language entries** — "aubergine" returns nothing useful even though the CNF carries French descriptions (`FoodDescriptionF`) for every food; (b) **compound descriptors** like "low-fat chocolate milk" — fuzzy ranking fails to combine attribute + flavor + commodity tokens; (c) **free-text dish names** like "homemade beef stew" or "spaghetti bolognese" — composite meals are not in CNF and cannot be entered at all without manual decomposition into individual CNF foods. AI-MATCH-1 surfaces two opt-in features that address all three, sharing the same RAG architecture the platform's environmental subsystem (§3.5) already used internally for CNF → Agribalyse matching.

**Feature 1 — Free-text → CNF FoodID matcher.** All 5,691 CNF foods are pre-embedded once via OpenAI's text-embedding-3-small (1,536-dim, L2-normalised; ~$0.005 one-time, ~30 s wall-clock; persisted to a 27.4 MB `.npz` with a `_provenance.json` sibling carrying the source-file SHA-256 for staleness detection at matcher startup). At query time the matcher ([`backend/api/services/cnf_matcher.py`](backend/api/services/cnf_matcher.py)) embeds the user query, picks the top-20 by cosine, then asks gpt-4.1-mini at temperature 0 to choose exactly one from the retrieved set with a calibrated-confidence score and a short justification. The seven validation gates mirror the production LCAMatcher pattern (§3.5): hallucination rejection (Krahmer 2024 LEAF precedent — the returned FoodID must be in the top-20 set), confidence threshold (default 0.6), graceful retrieval-only fallback when no LLM key is configured, and a per-process LRU cache (size 2,000) keyed on the normalised query string. The endpoint `POST /api/cnf/search/ai-enhanced/` is **additive** — the basic fuzzywuzzy search at `/api/cnf/search/` is unchanged; AI ranking is invoked only when the user clicks an explicit "Find with AI" button on the frontend.

**Feature 2 — Recipe decomposer.** A second class ([`backend/api/services/cnf_recipe_decomposer.py`](backend/api/services/cnf_recipe_decomposer.py)) takes a free-text dish name + total mass and returns a CNF ingredient list with per-ingredient masses, ready to feed the HENI / HEFI / HSR / FCS scoring endpoints. The pipeline is two-stage: (1) gpt-4.1-mini decomposes the dish into a free-text ingredient list with mass proportions (different from the LCA-side decomposer's constrained-vocabulary approach, since CNF is 5,691 entries and a single retrieval pool would not cover all canonical ingredients of, say, "spaghetti bolognese"); (2) each free-text ingredient name is resolved via Feature 1's `CNFMatcher` to a CNF FoodID with its own confidence score. Stage 2 runs concurrently via a ``ThreadPoolExecutor`` (max 8 workers; OpenAI SDK is documented thread-safe and both `CNFMatcher` caches use `threading.Lock`), dropping the wall-clock for a 6-ingredient recipe from ~12 s to ~5-6 s and for an 8-ingredient recipe to ~5 s — well inside a normal "press-button-see-result" UX. Four hard validation gates (min ingredients ≥ 2, mass closure within ±max(10 g, 4 %), decomposition confidence ≥ 0.30, no hallucinated CNF FoodIDs) and one soft gate (top-2-by-mass keyword anchor) ensure the output is publication-defensible. Ingredients that fail Stage-2 resolution (matcher confidence below floor) are excluded but logged in a per-decomposition audit trail; if more than 10 % of mass is unresolvable the harness returns `fallback_reason='partial_resolution'` rather than silently dropping content. A free-text `unresolved_description` field (populated by the Stage-1 LLM) explains what the residual IS — e.g. "butter or oil used for grilling" rather than a bare "10 g unresolved" — preventing the silent-residual ambiguity that small nutrition leaks (45 kcal of cooking butter dropped into an opaque residual) would otherwise hide. Endpoint `POST /api/recipes/decompose/` is similarly opt-in; the frontend exposes it via a "Score a homemade dish" modal on each of the four calculate pages, with editable per-row mass + per-row "swap" actions that re-use Feature 1's matcher.

**Stage-1 prompt rules.** The Stage-1 decomposition prompt carries four explicit rules added during AI-MATCH-1.x prompt refinement, each addressing a real failure mode observed in smoke runs against gpt-4.1-mini at temperature = 0: (a) **cautious-defaults** — prefer the generic unqualified CNF entry (e.g. "Tomato sauce" rather than "Tomato sauce, no salt added") unless the dish name explicitly calls for the variant, preventing gratuitous under-counting of sodium / fat / sugar; (b) **compound-dish** — "X with Y" / "X and Y" / "X on Y" constructions MUST produce both X and Y as explicit ingredients with non-zero mass, blocking the failure mode where the LLM bundles a named secondary into unresolved mass; (c) **specificity** — collective terms ("mixed berries", "leafy greens") must resolve to a representative single CNF entry ("blueberry", "spinach"), keeping Stage-2 above the 0.6 resolution-confidence floor; (d) **cooking-fat inclusion** — defining cooking fats (butter on grilled cheese, oil in stir-fry) appear as explicit ingredients with their typical proportion (3-10 % of total mass), preventing material nutrition leaks (a 10 g butter pat carries 45 kcal of saturated fat). Each rule is documented inline in the system prompt; their order matters because earlier rules can be defeated by later rules without explicit conditioning, and the prompt was iterated until the smoke suite stabilised.

**Cost / abuse / reproducibility controls.** Both endpoints share a per-IP hourly rate limit (50 requests / IP / hour default) plus a global monthly spend circuit breaker ($50 / month default) enforced via Django's cache framework. Decompose calls count as 5× a basic AI search against the budget to reflect their two-stage LLM cost (~$0.005 vs ~$0.001). Both endpoints are audience-aware: individual mode hides the LLM justification, per-ingredient resolution confidence, and audit trail; researcher and policy modes see everything. Per-query / per-(dish_name, mass) caching with temperature = 0 + normalised cache keys ensures reproducibility for downstream scoring.

**Validation.** Three smoke harnesses ground the rollout, layered for different drift modes. [`backend/_smoke_cnf_matcher.py`](backend/_smoke_cnf_matcher.py) runs a 4-panel × 10-query battery (canonical names, synonyms / foreign-language, compound descriptors, brand / fusion / recipe-style); Panel A is the gating panel and passes 10/10, with the remaining three panels documenting accuracy on each query class — overall **36/40 PASS** on the 2026-05-23 run, with the four misses (mangetout, aniseed, "kale chips", "avocado toast") reflecting real CNF coverage limits (CNF carries the ingredients, not the composite snacks) rather than matcher bugs. [`backend/_smoke_cnf_recipe_decomposer.py`](backend/_smoke_cnf_recipe_decomposer.py) runs 15 canonical recipes across cuisine (spaghetti bolognese, pad thai, jambalaya, shakshuka, …), simple (peanut butter sandwich, scrambled eggs + toast, grilled cheese, …), and adversarial (Buddha bowl, leftover Thanksgiving plate, homemade chicken soup) panels and asserts all 5 validation gates per recipe: **14/15 PASS**; the one flake is pad thai (a 10-ingredient dish whose post-cooking-fat-rule mass arithmetic noise lands ~10-14 g over a 320 g target on some runs), which would be brittle to fix in the prompt without breaking adjacent cases. The CNFRecipeDecomposer output for "spaghetti bolognese 300 g" decomposes to 6 ingredients (150 g cooked spaghetti, 80 g ground beef, 40 g tomato sauce, 10 g onion, 10 g carrot, 5 g olive oil, 5 g unresolved residual) at decomposition confidence 0.75 — well above the 0.30 floor, with each ingredient's Stage-2 resolution confidence ≥ 0.80. Finally [`backend/_smoke_cnf_recipe_decomposer_golden.py`](backend/_smoke_cnf_recipe_decomposer_golden.py) pins 3 stable recipes (peanut butter sandwich, scrambled eggs + toast, grilled cheese sandwich) against the gpt-4.1-mini snapshot captured 2026-05-23, asserting 6 gates per recipe: ingredient-set overlap ≥ 70 % of baseline FoodIDs, ingredient-count drift within ±1, total-mass drift within ±10 g, per-FoodID mass drift within ±10 g, `matched=True` after all decomposer gates, and `unresolved_description` populated when residual mass > 0. **3/3 PASS** as of 2026-05-23. This is the dedicated silent-drift detector: if OpenAI quietly updates the gpt-4.1-mini snapshot or someone tweaks the Stage-1 system prompt, the directional panels' loose keyword anchors might still pass — the golden harness will not, forcing explicit re-pinning rather than silent regression. All seven pre-existing smoke harnesses remain green (AUDIENCE-CODE-1 52/52, HSR canonical 9/9, HSR categorisation 36/36 + sweep 0/575 anomalies, FCS 11/11, HEFI 3/3, HENI 10/10).

**Feature 3 — 24-hour dietary recall wizard *(AI-MATCH-2, 2026-05-24; PKG-RECALL-1 packaged-food scan, 2026-05-26; direct CNF entry + history edit, 2026-05-28)*.** Feature 2 scores one dish at a time, but the validated diet-level metrics that consume CNF FoodID lists are *daily* by construction: HEFI-2019 was built explicitly against CCHS-Nutrition 24-h recall data (Brassard et al. 2022b Methods) and its mandatory single-day caveat — "*a single individual HEFI-2019 from one 24-h recall does NOT reflect usual adherence and must be interpreted with great caution*" (Brassard 2022b Discussion p. 588) — exists precisely because the index is designed for recall-scale inputs, not per-meal point estimates. HENI similarly aggregates marginal per-serving healthy-life-minutes across a real day's eating, and FCS's diet-level metric (i.FCS, O'Hearn 2022 *Nat Comm* 13:7066) is the energy-weighted mean Food Compass Score across daily intake. Without a recall-builder, users wishing to score their day against HEFI / HENI either (a) lose the daily aggregation by scoring individual meals separately, or (b) manually enumerate 20+ CNF FoodIDs — which nobody does. AI-MATCH-2 ships a guided occasion-by-occasion 24-h recall wizard ([`/recall-24h`](frontend/src/app/recall-24h/page.tsx)) that walks users through the six standard meal occasions (breakfast / AM snack / lunch / PM snack / dinner / evening snack), composes Feature 2's per-meal decomposer once per enabled occasion, and aggregates the per-meal CNF ingredient lists into a single daily list (deduped by FoodID with masses summed across meals — coffee at breakfast + coffee at the AM snack collapses to one row, preserving per-occasion attribution for researcher-mode audit). **Three entry modes per occasion (2026-05-28):** (i) free-text dish description (Feature 2 decomposer); (ii) **Pick ingredients** — debounced CNF search plus AI-enhanced lookup for users who already know what they ate (`entry_type: 'direct'`, routed through the same recall orchestrator as packaged meals); (iii) **Scan packaged food** — photograph the back-of-pack label and run the §3.8.7 multimodal extraction + CNF decomposition pipeline inline via [`PackagedFoodOccasionEntry`](frontend/src/components/shared/PackagedFoodOccasionEntry.tsx). Step 3 exposes a **Daily ingredients** editor so users can correct masses or add foods before scoring; saved days can be **re-opened and edited** from [`/recall-history`](frontend/src/app/recall-history/page.tsx) without rebuilding the wizard from scratch. This is the architecturally correct home for HEFI / HENI / FCS scoring of packaged products: Brassard 2022b built HEFI against 24-h recall data, not against single-product scans. The standalone `/scan-product` page remains **HSR-only** (photograph NF panel → review → score); decomposition and multi-scorer routing were deliberately removed from that page once PKG-RECALL-1 landed, eliminating duplicate UX and the misleading "score this one product as your whole day" handoff. The aggregated list routes via five 1-line adapter functions to any of HEFI / HENI / HSR / FCS / Environmental scoring endpoints with the right per-endpoint request shape. Throughput-critical: the orchestrator ([`backend/api/services/cnf_recall_24h.py`](backend/api/services/cnf_recall_24h.py)) runs text meals concurrently via a ThreadPoolExecutor (capped at 6 workers = one per occasion), so a mixed day (3 text meals + 1 scanned packaged snack) completes in ~max(per-meal latency) ≈ 6-8 s rather than 4 × sequential decomposes. Endpoint `POST /api/recipes/recall-24h/` shares the same per-IP rate limit + monthly circuit breaker as Features 1 + 2, with cost = 5¢/text-meal capped at 30¢ per recall (pre-decomposed packaged and direct-entry meals incur no additional per-meal LLM decompose charge). Aggregate-level sanity warnings (no breakfast/lunch/dinner logged, daily kcal below 800 or above 5000, `packaged_food_inferred_at_N_occasion(s)` when label scans are present) surface as amber nudges, never block. HSR daily aggregation is supported but flagged inline with a per-button warning explaining HSRAC v9 is a per-product within-category rating — daily HSR is mathematically computable but methodologically suspect; HEFI is the recommended index for 24-h recall data and the Brassard 2022b single-day caveat surfaces automatically when recall data routes there (already shipped via [`hefi_explanations.py:97-108`](backend/api/views/hefi_explanations.py#L97-L108)). One real interaction surfaced by the recall harness: single-food snacks like "banana" or "almonds" trip the per-dish decomposer's `min_ingredients ≥ 2` gate; the orchestrator handles this with a single-food fallback that routes the snack name through `CNFMatcher.match()` directly and synthesises a 1-ingredient `CNFDecomposedRecipe`. Two validation harnesses ground the rollout: [`backend/_smoke_cnf_recall_24h.py`](backend/_smoke_cnf_recall_24h.py) runs 5 canonical daily-eating patterns (sedentary, active, vegetarian, high-snack, adversarial weekend-brunch) × 7 gates each (all-meals-decomposed, kcal in [800, 5000], ≥ 3 food groups, per-meal mass closure, no hallucinated FoodIDs, HEFI routing, HENI routing) — **5/5 daily patterns PASS all 7 gates** on the 2026-05-24 run, with HEFI scores landing in [10, 70] and HENI returning finite healthy-life-minute impacts for every recall. [`backend/_smoke_cnf_recall_24h_golden.py`](backend/_smoke_cnf_recall_24h_golden.py) pins a 3-meal day (peanut butter sandwich + scrambled eggs with toast + grilled cheese sandwich) against the gpt-4.1-mini snapshot captured 2026-05-24, asserting 6 gates at the aggregate level: recall matched=True, aggregated FoodID overlap ≥ 70 %, count drift ±2, total-mass drift ±15 g, per-FoodID mass drift ±15 g, daily kcal drift ±15 % — slightly looser tolerances than the per-dish golden harness to absorb cumulative cooking-fat-rule overshoot across meals. **1/1 PASS** on the 2026-05-24 run (overlap 100 %, count 6/6, mass drift 2 g, kcal drift 1.7 %). All ten pre-existing smoke harnesses remain green.

### 3.8.5 WAFCT 2019 cross-database integration *(WAFCT-EXTEND, 2026-05-24)*

The Canadian Nutrient File (CNF, 5,691 foods) was authoritative for the Canadian audience we built against, but carries near-zero coverage of staple West African foods. A user in Lagos or Accra scoring "jollof rice", "fonio porridge", or "baobab-leaf sauce" against HEFI/HENI/HSR/FCS gets no useful output from a CNF-only catalog — the ingredients simply aren't in the database. We integrated the FAO/INFOODS West African Food Composition Table (WAFCT 2019, [Vincent, Grande et al. 2019](https://www.fao.org/infoods/infoods/tables-and-databases/faoinfoods-databases/en/), CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO) as a second cross-database food source — 1,028 foods × 39-57 nutrients per 100 g edible portion across 14 food groups, plus 195 cross-referenced canonical recipes (sheet 09) and 467 bibliographic sources. The exploration study that anchored the integration choice is fully documented at [`WAFCT_EXPLORATION.md`](WAFCT_EXPLORATION.md); two read-only harnesses ([`backend/_explore_wafct.py`](backend/_explore_wafct.py) + [`backend/_explore_wafct_vs_cnf_per100g.py`](backend/_explore_wafct_vs_cnf_per100g.py)) drove the structural inventory + per-100g empirical comparison.

**Architecture: Option B (WAFCT-as-extension with a `source` column).** Among three integration candidates analysed (source-tagged unification with namespaced FoodIDs; WAFCT-as-extension; bridge-table), Option B has the lowest blast radius — WAFCT foods get FoodIDs offset by 700,000 (CNF max is 503,381 — clean headroom); their nutrient values are translated INFOODS-tag → CNF NutrientID at ingest via a 47-entry programmatic bridge built from CNF's existing `Tagname` column plus a tiny alias map for the ~6 tags where CNF deviates from INFOODS naming (`PROCNT` ↔ `PROTCNT`, `CHOCDF` ↔ `CHOAVLDF`, `ENERC_KCAL` ↔ `ENERC` + kcal unit, etc.); a `source ∈ {cnf, wafct}` column on `food_name_df` preserves provenance. The key payoff: **HEFI / HENI / HSR / FCS / Environmental scoring code is unchanged**. Every scorer goes through `pipeline.nutrients_for(food_id) → {NutrientName: float}`, which works identically for either source once the bridge translates WAFCT INFOODS values into CNF-keyed cells. Ingest runs once per process from [`api.cnf_cache.get_api_cnf_pipeline()`](backend/api/cnf_cache.py); graceful-degrades if the WAFCT workbook is absent.

**Empirical bridge findings.** The per-100g study ([`backend/_explore_wafct_vs_cnf_per100g.py`](backend/_explore_wafct_vs_cnf_per100g.py), 9 paired foods × 10 shared nutrients) found macronutrients agree well (Energy, Water, Protein, Fat, Carbs, Fibre median |Δ%| ≤ 13 %, no systematic bias direction) but **minerals show a consistent WAFCT-higher pattern: Ca +23.5 %, Fe +67.7 %, Mg +15.6 %, K +10.8 %** (median Δ% across the 9 pairs). Likely causes are soil composition (West African soils carry different mineral profiles), traditional cookware effects (iron pots demonstrably elevate cooked-food iron), and FAO-INFOODS-vs-Health-Canada analytical-method differences — none of which we attempt to correct numerically. Instead a per-source caveat surfaces in researcher / policy mode whenever a WAFCT food is in the meal, citing the per-nutrient bias table directly + naming the indicator-specific risk (HEFI's free-sugars component is biased because WAFCT lacks SUGAR / SUGARS_FREE; HSR's sodium component picks up the mineral bias; HENI flags iron-zinc bioavailability discounting as un-modelled because the v1 integration drops WAFCT's phytate / inositol-phosphate columns; FCS notes the generic mineral bias). Individual mode receives a plain-language one-liner. Environmental impact is unit-agnostic to nutrient analytical method — no caveat needed.

**User-visible surface.** A new [`SourceFilter`](frontend/src/components/shared/SourceFilter.tsx) segmented control (`Source: [Both] [CNF] [WAFCT]`) sits above the food-search box on all five calculator pages (HEFI / HENI / HSR / FCS / Environmental), defaults to "Both", and persists per-search. The calculators' autocomplete path prefers `GET /api/search-food/`; until **2026-05-25** that legacy endpoint still queried CNF-only `FOOD_NAME.csv` with fuzzywuzzy relevance and silently ignored `source`, leaking North-American noise (example: tofu near-matches for `"fufu"`). It now resolves through `get_dish_cnf_pipeline().search_foods(query, ..., source=…)` on the same merged in-memory corpus as `GET /api/cnf/search/` ([`cnf_views.py`](backend/api/views/cnf_views.py)), so `source=wafct` returns FoodIDs ≥ 700,000 exclusively. The matcher's corpus rebuild merged CNF + WAFCT into a single 6,719-row embedding store ([`build_cnf_corpus_embeddings.py`](backend/api/services/etl/build_cnf_corpus_embeddings.py), 35.5 s rebuild at $0.005 OpenAI cost); `CNFMatcher.match(query, source='wafct')` filters the candidate pool BEFORE LLM ranking so the model only sees in-scope foods. Per-result provenance badges ([`SourceBadge`](frontend/src/components/shared/SourceBadge.tsx)) flag WAFCT rows in researcher / policy mode. The 24-h recall wizard inherits WAFCT support transparently because it composes the matcher + decomposer with no source-awareness needed at the orchestrator level.

**Validation.** [`backend/_smoke_wafct_integration.py`](backend/_smoke_wafct_integration.py) runs 6 directional gates: G1 ingest succeeds (6,719 total foods = 5,691 CNF + 1,028 WAFCT); G2 source column populated; G3 nutrient lookup works for WAFCT FoodIDs (5 sampled, all return CNF-keyed nutrient names within plausible per-100g kcal bounds); G4 matcher returns WAFCT for WAFCT-only queries (`fonio porridge` → WAFCT 700023 at confidence 0.80; `baobab leaves` → WAFCT 700420 at 0.85; `dawadawa fermented locust bean` → WAFCT 700280 / African locust bean soumbala at 0.85); G5 source filter respected (query `whole milk` with `source='cnf'` returns CNF 113 / 3.25% MF; `source='wafct'` returns WAFCT 700902 / 3.5% fat); G6 end-to-end scoring for a WAFCT-only 3-food meal returns HEFI 30.0 / HENI +5.2 healthy-life-minute impact + the `wafct_caveat` block surfaces with `wafct_food_count_in_meal=3`. **6/6 PASS** on the 2026-05-24 run. All 12 pre-existing smoke harnesses remain green (matcher 36/40, recipe decomposer 14/15, decomposer golden 3/3, recall-24h 5/5, recall-24h golden 1/1 at overlap 100 % / mass drift 1 g / kcal drift 1.0 %, audience contract 52/52, HSR canonical 9/9, HSR categorisation 36/36, HSR sweep 0 anomalies, FCS canonical, HEFI canonical 3/3, HENI literature panel 10/10). Cold-start cost: ~2 s pipeline + matcher load with WAFCT included (was ~1 s CNF-only).

**Out of scope (v1).** WAFCT mixed-dish recipes (sheet 09's 195 canonical West African recipes are structurally cross-referenced by WAFCT Code — wiring them as a deterministic-lookup tier in front of the LLM decomposer is a follow-up plan); WAFCT-only nutrient extensions (phytate / IP3-6 bioavailability discounting for HENI / FCS); CNF↔WAFCT full-corpus auto-matching for equivalent foods (would be a one-shot ~$2-3 OpenAI cost); FoodEx2 (sheet 10, 90.9 % WAFCT coverage) as a deterministic cross-source bridge.

### 3.8.6 Dietary-pattern resemblance via embedding similarity *(DIET-PATTERN-1, 2026-05-24)*

The five scoring indicators above (HEFI, FCS, HSR, HENI, environmental) all answer "*how* well is this meal / day eating?" with a number. None answers "*what kind* of eating is this?" — the categorical / qualitative descriptor (Mediterranean, DASH, Western, Vegetarian) that nutrition counselling actually uses. The literature offers three meanings for "dietary pattern" — a priori indices (HEFI / FCS are exactly that), a posteriori PCA / RRR (Hu 2002 *Curr Opin Lipidol* 13:3 + Schulze 2003 *J Nutr* 134:1793; requires population-scale cohort data we don't have access to), and reference-pattern resemblance (Mediterranean = Trichopoulou 2003 *NEJM* 348:2599 + Estruch 2013 PREDIMED *NEJM* 368:1279; DASH = Sacks 2001 *NEJM* 344:3; Vegetarian / Vegan = Orlich 2013 *JAMA Intern Med* 173:1230). [`DIETARY_PATTERN_JUSTIFICATION.md`](DIETARY_PATTERN_JUSTIFICATION.md) reviewed the extracted literature (B6-B12, C15-C21, D22-D27) and recommended the third path because it is the only one that reuses our embedding + recall infrastructure cleanly and the only one that can characterise a single individual's day. **The architectural novelty** — and the manuscript justification for §3.8.6 alongside §3.5 (matcher) / §3.8 (AI-MATCH) — is that embedding-space similarity has been published for food→food matching ([NutriRAG D23 Zhou 2025](literature_extractions.md#L2520)) but **not** for dietary-pattern resemblance. Mass-weighted day vectors compared by cosine to literature-anchored prototype vectors is a clean composition of techniques the literature has used separately.

**Architecture.** A new `DietaryPatternMatcher` ([`backend/api/services/dietary_pattern.py`](backend/api/services/dietary_pattern.py)) takes an aggregated daily ingredient list (typically from the [Recall24hWizard](frontend/src/components/shared/Recall24hWizard.tsx) via the existing `useRecall24hReceiver` hand-off), looks up each food's 1,536-dim embedding from the shipped [`cnf_corpus_embeddings.npz`](backend/api/data/cnf_corpus_embeddings.npz) (6,719 foods covering CNF + WAFCT after WAFCT-EXTEND), computes a mass-weighted L2-normalised day vector, and reports cosine similarity to each prototype's vector (mean of curated example-day vectors). Softmax-T=0.1 produces a probability-like share; confidence bands (`high` if top cosine ≥ 0.75 with ≥ 0.05 gap to runner-up, `moderate` if ≥ 0.60, `low` otherwise) gate the strength of the user-facing claim; co-leading patterns within 0.05 cosine of the top are reported jointly to avoid spurious tie-breaking. Per-prototype "distinctive foods you ate" surfaces the user's top-3 foods that contributed most to that prototype's score (mass × cosine to the prototype vector). Zero new ML, zero new ETL — the matcher composes existing primitives.

**Prototype library.** Seven prototypes default-visible in all audience modes (Mediterranean, DASH, Western / Standard American, Vegetarian, Vegan, CFG-Healthy, West African Staple), plus an optional 8th (EAT-Lancet planetary-health, Willett 2019 *Lancet* 393:447) visible only in researcher / policy mode. Each prototype carries 2-3 hand-curated example days with mandatory `literature_anchor` + `outcome_evidence_reused` JSON fields citing the source paper section ([`backend/api/data/dietary_pattern_prototypes.json`](backend/api/data/dietary_pattern_prototypes.json), 23 example days across 8 prototypes anchored to Trichopoulou 2003 / Estruch 2013 PREDIMED, Sacks 2001 / Appel 1997 DASH, Krebs-Smith 2010 NHANES, Orlich 2013 AHS-2, Brassard 2022a HEFI components, Vincent 2019 WAFCT sheet 09 canonical recipes, Willett 2019). West African Staple is the cross-database payoff of the WAFCT-EXTEND integration — a WAFCT canonical day (fonio + jollof rice + baobab-leaf sauce) scores 96.7 % resemblance to its own prototype and is correctly distinguished from CNF-based patterns even in the discriminative individual-mode space.

**Honest framing.** The user never sees a single binary label — always the full top-3 resemblance vector with cosine bars, the "co-leading" annotation when patterns sit within 0.05 of each other, and the mandatory single-day caveat reused verbatim from the HEFI-2019 [`hefi_explanations.py`](backend/api/views/hefi_explanations.py) Brassard 2022b discipline ("today's day-vector is one snapshot; for usual-eating-pattern claims, log multiple recall days"). Outcome citations attached to each prototype (Estruch 2013 CVD HR 0.70, Orlich 2013 vegan all-cause mortality HR 0.85, etc.) are reused-by-reference, never computed — researcher mode shows the citation alongside the explicit caveat that "outcome citations refer to populations following each pattern long-term, NOT to single-day resemblance". The optional LLM narrative (opt-in, `include_narrative=true`, +1¢/call) is prompt-rule-constrained to forbid "you eat" / "you are eating" framing in favour of "today's food choices resemble".

**Endpoint, frontend, validation.** `POST /api/dietary-pattern/classify/` shares the same per-IP rate limit + monthly circuit breaker as the §3.4-§3.8 endpoints (cost 1¢ baseline, 2¢ with narrative). A new `/dietary-pattern` page ([`frontend/src/app/dietary-pattern/page.tsx`](frontend/src/app/dietary-pattern/page.tsx)) receives the recall payload via the existing `useRecall24hReceiver` sessionStorage mechanism (extending its `Target` union with `'dietary_pattern'`); the wizard's Step 4 score-routing gains a 6th button "🎯 Score Dietary Pattern". [`backend/_smoke_dietary_pattern.py`](backend/_smoke_dietary_pattern.py) runs 6 directional gates: G1 self-match scoped to the 7 individual-mode prototypes (expected in top-1 or co-leading) — **21/21 pass** (20 strict top-1 + 1 co-leading); G2 cross-distinguishability — **median cross-prototype cosine 0.827, ≤ 0.92 threshold**, EAT-Lancet excluded as the planetary-health composite it is by design; G3 known-pattern reference days — **10/10 correct** (Greek-village → Mediterranean, Indian thali → Vegetarian, BBQ pulled-pork → Western, WAFCT canonical → West African Staple, vegan stir-fry → Vegan, DASH-style → DASH, CFG plate → CFG-Healthy, EAT-Lancet reference → EAT-Lancet, Italian → Mediterranean, fast-food → Western); G4 WAFCT-aware sanity — WAFCT canonical day → `west_african_staple` at cosine 0.967; G5 portion-scale invariance — top-3 ranking stable across 0.5x / 2.0x / 5.0x mass scaling (mass-weighted-then-L2-normalised is mass-invariant by construction); G6 degenerate inputs handled — empty list, single food, all-zero-mass, missing FoodIDs all return meaningful fallback reasons without exceptions. **6/6 gates PASS** on the 2026-05-24 run. All 13 pre-existing smoke harnesses remain green (recall-24h golden 1/1 at overlap 100 %, mass drift 0 g, kcal drift 0.0 %).

**Out of scope (v1).** A posteriori PCA/RRR pattern derivation from CCHS Public-Use Microdata (blocked on RDC access, deferred to a separate plan); new a priori pattern index ("Pattern-HEFI") — would add an un-validated scoring rubric the pipeline doesn't need; outcome-prediction modelling at the user level (the platform doesn't collect health-outcome data and shouldn't pretend to predict them — outcome citations are reused-by-reference, not computed); ~~persistent per-user pattern history~~ (now shipped, see next paragraph); ~~substantive swap-recommendation engine beyond the LLM narrative's casual third-sentence suggestion~~ (now shipped as SUBST-1 §3.8.8); custom user-defined prototypes; SVG radar chart (v1 uses horizontal cosine bars — polygonal radar is a v1.x polish).

**Multi-day usual-intake support (RECALL-HISTORY-1, 2026-05-24).** The universal multi-day caveat the pipeline appends to every single-day pattern, HEFI, HENI, and FCS result ("today's day-vector is one snapshot; for usual-eating-pattern claims, log multiple recall days") was a UX-discipline gap until shipping: the platform asked for a behaviour the UI did not actually support. We close the gap with a localStorage-based recall-history surface ([`/recall-history`](frontend/src/app/recall-history/page.tsx), [`recallHistory.ts`](frontend/src/lib/recallHistory.ts), per-browser, no auth, no backend persistence) that lets users save individual recall days from the wizard's Step 3 Review screen, view a per-day pattern distribution across all saved days, and compute an N-day average pattern by mass-weighted concatenation of the selected days' aggregated ingredient lists routed through the same `/api/dietary-pattern/classify/` endpoint (`combineDays()` aggregates by FoodID; `meta_label` request field triggers the backend to substitute the softened "begins to approximate your usual eating but is still based on a limited sample" caveat for the single-day disclaimer). Full JSON / CSV export (`exportAsJSON`, `exportAsCSV` — RFC 4180 quoted, one CSV row per (day, meal, food) tuple) supports researcher workflows for offline analysis in R / Python / SPSS; JSON import round-trips for cross-device transfer. **The true NCI multivariate MCMC usual-intake method (Zhang 2011) remains out of scope** — the N-day-average view is a defensible volume-weighted approximation that captures the directional intent of multi-day averaging (and is forthrightly framed as such in the receiving page's caveat block) without claiming the statistical machinery. No backend persistence; no auth.

### 3.8.7 Multimodal label-image extraction for packaged foods *(PKG-IMG-1, 2026-05-26; PKG-RECALL-1 integration, 2026-05-26)*

The five non-HSR scorers (HEFI / HENI / FCS / dietary-pattern / environmental) all consume Canadian Nutrient File FoodIDs plus per-food masses. For users wanting to score *packaged* products — most branded retail goods, which are not in CNF as discrete entries — every path before PKG-IMG-1 required manually typing 7–9 Nutrition Facts macros and free-text-entering an ingredient list. HSR was the worst case (designed exactly for packaged foods; HSRAC v9 cell-by-cell-pinned per §3.2; 9/9 canonical-panel smoke pass) yet still required the manual macro entry. PKG-IMG-1 closes the friction gap with a multimodal-LLM extraction pipeline: photograph the label, review the prefilled values, score. **Architectural split (PKG-RECALL-1, 2026-05-26):** the standalone `/scan-product` page is now **HSR-only** — `POST /api/packaged-food/extract/` (NF panel) → user review → `POST /api/hsr/calculate-from-panel/`. Ingredient-list extraction, CNF decomposition, and routing to HEFI / HENI / FCS / dietary-pattern / environmental scorers live exclusively in the **24-h recall wizard** (§3.8 Feature 3), where a scanned packaged food logs as one occasion (breakfast, AM snack, etc.) and aggregates with text-described meals before daily scoring. This directly operationalises Barrett et al. (2025)'s call for LLM-assisted ingredient-list interpretation *within* a recall-scale input — the unit HEFI-2019 was validated on (Brassard 2022b) — rather than treating a single branded product as a proxy for an entire day.

**Architecture — extraction.** `POST /api/packaged-food/extract/` (HSR path; ~1¢) and `POST /api/packaged-food/extract-combined/` (recall path; NF + ingredient list; ~1¢) ([`packaged_food_views.py`](backend/api/views/packaged_food_views.py)) take multipart image uploads, run HEIC/AVIF → JPEG normalisation + 1600-px-long-edge downscale + SHA-256 hashing in [`multimodal_client.py`](backend/api/services/multimodal_client.py), and call the multimodal LLM. For HSR the user reviews + confirms the panel + selects an HSRAC v9 category + supplies an estimated FVNL %, then `POST /api/hsr/calculate-from-panel/` constructs an `HSRFood(food_id=0, food_name=...)` synthetic entry directly from the panel macros (bypassing the CNF lookup the existing HSR view assumes) and routes through the rust_core HSR engine unchanged — no new HSR math. For recall-integrated scoring, `POST /api/packaged-food/decompose-ingredients/` ([`ingredient_to_cnf_decomposer.py`](backend/api/services/ingredient_to_cnf_decomposer.py), ~2¢ at text-only LLM) runs a three-stage pipeline: (Stage 1) per-ingredient top-5 CNF candidate retrieval via the existing `CNFMatcher.match()`, aggregated into a constrained candidate pool; (Stage 2) text-only constrained LLM mapping prompted with the panel macros + per-ingredient candidates + net weight, with hard constraints — `food_id` must come from the candidate pool, sum of inferred masses must reconcile with `net_weight ± 5 %`, resulting macros must reconcile with the NF panel `± 10 %` per macro, ingredient ordering must respect descending mass; (Stage 3) server-side validation lowers `decomposition_confidence` for violations and surfaces them in `decomposition_warnings`. Pre-decomposed results fold into `POST /api/recipes/recall-24h/` via `entry_type: 'packaged'` (§3.8 Feature 3).

**Multiprovider model routing.** Embeddings for CNF candidate retrieval remain OpenAI-only (`text-embedding-3-small`; Anthropic exposes no embedding API). Vision extraction and text JSON decomposition share a provider switch (`LLM_PROVIDER=anthropic`) with separate model overrides: `MULTIMODAL_LLM_MODEL=claude-opus-4-7` for label-image extraction, `CHAT_LLM_MODEL=claude-opus-4-7` for ingredient decomposition and CNF ranking. Production default remains `gpt-4o-mini` / `gpt-4.1-mini` for cost; Opus is opt-in via environment configuration. Opus 4.7 rejects legacy Anthropic API parameters (`temperature`, assistant prefill for JSON); the client layer retries without them — a provider-capability pattern now shared between [`multimodal_client.py`](backend/api/services/multimodal_client.py) and [`llm_client.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/llm_client.py).

**Schema discipline + post-hoc normaliser.** The Pydantic schema [`packaged_food_schema.py`](backend/api/services/packaged_food_schema.py) is intentionally strict (`extra="forbid"` on every model). Each numeric field is its own object: `{value, unit, confidence, raw_text, from_dv_percent, from_kcal_conversion}`. We observed empirically that multimodal LLMs drift to flatter shapes (`{calories: 110}` instead of `{per_serving: {energy_kcal: {value: 110, unit: 'kcal', ...}}}`) and to alternate sub-key names (`{numeric_value: 250, units: 'mL'}` instead of `{value: 250, unit: 'ml'}`). Rather than tighten the prompt (which historically degrades on edge cases — see §3.8.7.1 below), we ship a post-hoc normaliser ([`packaged_food_extractor.py`](backend/api/services/packaged_food_extractor.py) `normalise_llm_extraction()`) that remaps observed flat-vs-nested patterns, merges vitamin/mineral sub-tables (Canadian infant-formula "Nutrition Information" panels), whitelists canonical NF keys, and applies an explicit alias dictionary (`_NUMERIC_SUBKEY_ALIASES`: 18+ known LLM aliases including `numeric_value→value`, `units→unit`, `carbohydrates_total_g→carbohydrate_total_g`) before Pydantic validation. Sanity-range guards then zero the confidence on physically-implausible values (sodium > 5 g/serving, energy > 2 000 kcal/serving, etc.) — preserving the value for user review rather than silently rejecting it. Production prompt is at **`PROMPT_VERSION=5`**, adding Rule K2 for Canadian infant-formula per-100 mL tables (`panel_format_detected: canadian_infant_formula`) and combined-path micronutrient parity with the NF-only prompt.

**Honest framing — what's measured vs inferred.** Regulation only requires ingredient lists to be in descending-mass-order, NOT to disclose percentages (the EU's QUID rules are the main exception). The Stage-2 decomposer is therefore producing *inferred* composition, not *measured* composition. The recall wizard surfaces this at scan confirmation ("composition inferred from label") and the backend adds `packaged_food_inferred_at_N_occasion(s)` to aggregate warnings when scanned meals are present. Receiving scorer pages carry `packaged_food_occasions` metadata via the existing `useRecall24hReceiver` sessionStorage handoff; all six Scorecard scorer endpoints accept `decomposition_provenance='packaged_food_inferred'` and merge an audience-aware `inferred_composition_caveat` into their explanations block (shared helper [`packaged_food_caveat.py`](backend/api/views/packaged_food_caveat.py)). The Scorecard page additionally renders an amber list-level banner when `packaged_food` metadata is present on the active food list.

#### 3.8.7.1 Methods comparison — prompt engineering vs post-hoc normaliser

The choice between **prompt-level robustness** (heavier prompt with worked examples, schema-as-code, and Chain-of-Verification — per Sahoo et al. 2024 *Systematic Survey of Prompt Engineering in Large Language Models*, arXiv:2402.07927v2) and **post-hoc normalisation** (a code-side coercion layer that accepts whatever shape the LLM naturally emits) is a real architectural choice with measurable trade-offs. We A/B-benchmarked the alternatives on our 5-image test panel ([`backend/_benchmark_prompt_variants.py`](backend/_benchmark_prompt_variants.py), 4 variants × 5 images × 3 runs = 60 LLM calls at gpt-4o-mini vision; configurable via `--runs`, `--variants`, `--images` flags):

- **V1** — current production (prompt v2 + normaliser ON)
- **V2** — current prompt v2 + normaliser OFF (isolates the normaliser's contribution)
- **V3** — enhanced prompt (one worked few-shot example + TypeScript-interface schema literal + 5-point CoVe self-verification checklist) + normaliser OFF
- **V4** — enhanced prompt + normaliser ON (belt-and-suspenders)

Final aggregate results on the 2026-05-26 run (full per-cell detail in [`backend/_benchmark_prompt_variants_results.json`](backend/_benchmark_prompt_variants_results.json); `PROMPT_VERSION=4` in production):

| Variant | Schema-OK pre-norm | Mean field accuracy [min–max] | Latency | Input/output tokens | Normaliser fired |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 (production) | 0 / 15 (0 %) | **88.2 %** [69–100 %] | 18.8 s | 3 274 / 500 | 15 / 15 |
| V2 (no normaliser) | 0 / 15 (0 %) | 0.0 % | 17.9 s | 3 274 / 470 | n/a |
| V3 (enhanced + no norm) | 12 / 15 (80 %) | 76.1 % [0–100 %] | 46.6 s | 5 975 / 1 129 | n/a |
| V4 (enhanced + norm) | 10 / 15 (67 %) | 63.2 % [0–100 %] | 31.8 s | 5 975 / 1 110 | 15 / 15 |

Four findings:

1. **The normaliser is load-bearing.** V2 (current prompt, no normaliser) is **0 %** — every one of 15 LLM outputs fails strict-schema validation pre-normalisation. The normaliser rescues all 15 outputs in V1 and the pipeline then achieves 88.2 % field accuracy. Removing the normaliser is not viable under the current production prompt.

2. **Prompt enhancement increases raw schema compliance from 0 % to 80 %** (the few-shot + schema-as-code intervention does what Sahoo et al. predict on the structurally well-behaved cases), and crucially **the catastrophic-failure pattern is now isolated to a single image type rather than two**. After the targeted prompt + normaliser bug fixes (carbohydrates_total_g plural-s alias added to the normaliser; combined-path user prompt brought to parity with the NF-only path's expanded critical-fields list including micronutrients), V3 on the Canadian bilingual curved-can photo recovered from 0 % to 100 % across all 3 runs. The FDA dual-column "per serving / per container" panel remains the lone catastrophic-failure case — the single-column granola-bar worked example biases the LLM into picking the wrong column on dual-column layouts, and CoVe self-verification fails to catch the resulting structurally-malformed JSON.

3. **Belt-and-suspenders (V4) can be WORSE than the enhanced prompt alone (V3).** V3 = 76.1 %, V4 = 63.2 %. The normaliser sometimes mangles structurally-correct outputs from the enhanced prompt — most visibly on the Chicken Noodle image where V3 scores 100 % but V4 only 33 %. The two layers are not strictly additive; the normaliser is tuned for the *production* prompt's output shape, and applying it to a differently-shaped enhanced-prompt output can be subtractive. Keep the layers paired only with V1's lighter prompt.

4. **Cost / latency overhead remains substantial**: V3/V4's enhanced prompt is +82.5 % input tokens and runs ~2.5–3× slower per call (V3 47 s vs V1 19 s) at gpt-4o-mini vision pricing.

Net verdict (applied by the harness via the plan's decision matrix): **keep V1**. The gap (V1 88.2 % vs V3 76.1 %, −12.1 pp net) is now driven entirely by the FDA dual-column case where V3 is 0/3 and V1 is 74 %. V3 has gained genuine wins on smaller cases but at the cost of one consistent catastrophic-failure mode. The benchmark file is retained in the repository as living documentation of the architectural choice and is rerunnable against future prompt designs.

**Ground-truth audit transparency.** Three sequential benchmark runs were required to land at trustworthy numbers. (a) A first 60-call run produced V1 = 80.1 % aggregate accuracy with the catastrophic-failure pattern on 2 of 5 images. (b) A careful per-image audit (visual re-extraction of each label against the smoke-harness's expected values) revealed 8 hand-transcription errors in the original ground truth: 6 on the small AVIF heat-and-eat pouch (serving 240→340 g, fat 1→2 g, sodium 480→470 mg, fibre 1→3 g, sugars 5→4 g, protein 18→16 g) and 2 on the bilingual Sea Family Foods tub (protein 5→8 g, sodium 600→510 mg). Both error-laden GT entries were on real-product photos with small panels (vs the synthetic marketing graphics that had been transcribed cleanly); the AI extractor had been correct on those fields, my hand-typed GT had been wrong. (c) A second targeted live-extraction audit on the production combined-prompt path then surfaced two real bugs: the LLM was emitting `carbohydrates_total_g` (plural-s) on the heavier combined prompt — a key the post-hoc normaliser's alias dictionary did not yet contain, silently dropping every Total Carbohydrate value before validation — and the `build_combined_user_prompt()` function had been emitting a terse field list that omitted the micronutrient + Total Carbohydrate emphasis the NF-only path had received in `PROMPT_VERSION=3`. A 4th run with the alias dictionary patched (`carbohydrates_total_g → carbohydrate_total_g`), the combined user prompt brought to parity, and `PROMPT_VERSION=4` cache-invalidating both, plus a final GT correction on the bilingual Sea Family Foods image (fibre 5→8 g, discovered when a high-resolution crop of the panel was matched against the % Daily Value column — 8g/28g Health Canada DV = 28.6 % ≈ 29 % printed), produced the V1 = 88.2 % numbers reported here. This is honest discipline: the audit caught one ground-truth bug per ~1.5 image entries and two real production bugs along the way.

**One unfixable model limitation discovered.** On the bilingual Sea Family Foods image, gpt-4o-mini's vision pathway consistently misreads "Fibre / Fibres 8 g" as "3 g" — the % Daily Value column (29 %) is consistent with 8 g but the model cannot resolve the digit shape reliably even after multiple re-runs. Separately, on the Canadian "Saturated / saturés 0.4 g + Trans / trans 0 g" combined row (Health Canada's standard typographic convention for the saturated + trans pair), the model extracts the saturated value but returns null for trans across all variants. Both are vision-model accuracy ceilings rather than prompt or schema issues — surfaced in §7.6 as known limits.

**Methodological takeaway** (cross-referenceable in §7.6 on AI-system limits): for structured extraction tasks where the LLM's structural drift varies across input types, a post-hoc normaliser is a more robust architecture than a heavier prompt. The normaliser can be incrementally extended as new alias patterns are observed (we have added 2 distinct shape variants to the alias dictionary since v1 launch, ~10 LoC each); a prompt-level intervention that wins on one input type can simultaneously catastrophically lose on another, with no incremental fix path.

#### 3.8.7.2 Empirical findings — model tier, label formats, and recall integration

Three live-test panels on real product photos (stored in `backend/packeged_foods_images/`) quantify what the extraction + decomposition pipeline can and cannot do as of `PROMPT_VERSION=5` (2026-05-26):

| Test image | Label type | Critical fields | gpt-4o-mini (combined) | claude-opus-4-7 (combined) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic soup (Sea Family Foods) | Canadian bilingual NF + ingredients | 7 macros + micronutrients | 7/7 stable (K=600 mg, carb=29 g after normaliser fix) | 7/7 |
| Gerber Good Start Plus 1 RTF | Canadian infant formula, per-100 mL table | 8 (energy, protein, fat, carb, Na, K, Ca, Fe) | 6–7/8 (K/Ca/Fe approximate) | **8/8 exact** (K=72, Ca=44, Fe=1 mg per 100 mL) |
| Campbell's condensed soup | Canadian bilingual curved-can | NF + 11-ingredient list | HSR path OK; decompose → 11 CNF foods | — |

Four methodological findings follow:

1. **The post-hoc normaliser is the breakthrough enabler, not the model tier alone.** Before the `carbohydrates_total_g` alias and combined-path micronutrient prompt parity fix (`PROMPT_VERSION=4→5`), potassium and total carbohydrate intermittently returned null on the combined extraction path even when visible on the label — a silent data-loss bug the strict Pydantic schema would have rejected entirely without the normaliser layer. After the fix, gpt-4o-mini reaches 7/7 on standard NF panels; the normaliser remains load-bearing (0 % schema-OK without it; §3.8.7.1).

2. **Model tier matters on out-of-distribution label formats.** Canadian infant-formula "Nutrition Information" tables (per-100 mL dense grids, not standard NF footers) are a distinct visual regime from HSRAC v9 training panels. Opus 4.7 reads all eight ground-truth fields exactly on Gerber Good Start Plus 1; gpt-4o-mini reaches 6–7/8 with approximate micronutrients. Prompt Rule K2 + `_merge_vitamin_mineral_table()` in the normaliser handle the nested table shape; the remaining gap on mini is vision-digit accuracy, not schema shape. This supports the multiprovider split: cost-optimised mini for mainstream NF panels, Opus opt-in for formula / compact / atypical layouts.

3. **Recall integration is the correct decomposition surface.** Scoring a single scanned product as an entire day (the Phase-2 `/scan-product` handoff) misaligned with HEFI's recall-scale validation (Brassard 2022b) and inflated inferred-composition risk when one product stood in for 20+ CNF foods. PKG-RECALL-1 moves decomposition into Step 2 of the recall wizard: the user can mix "scanned granola bar at AM snack" + "homemade stew at dinner" in one aggregated day, with per-occasion `packaged_food_inferred` attribution preserved through FoodID dedup. The backend accepts pre-decomposed packaged meals on `POST /api/recipes/recall-24h/` without re-running the dish decomposer; rate-limit cost applies to text meals only.

4. **Net-weight anchoring remains the binding constraint for decomposition.** Infant-formula RTF labels often lack a gram net weight (serving size in mL only). Decomposition requires a mass-conservation anchor; without user-supplied net weight the pipeline returns `no_net_weight` and refuses to invent masses. This is intentional — inferred composition without an anchor would be unconstrained fiction. The recall UI exposes an editable net-weight field at scan confirmation.

**Out of scope (Phase 3 PENDING).** Barcode + Open Food Facts fast path: frontend `@zxing/library` barcode detection in-browser (Apache 2.0, no API key) plus a backend `/api/packaged-food/barcode-lookup/` proxying to Open Food Facts (~3 M products, CC-BY-SA) with Django-cached lookups, falling through to the multimodal LLM path on miss. Cuts per-scan cost to ~0¢ on barcode hits. Gates: Phase 2 G4/G5/G6 PASS + informal Canadian-retail barcode-hit-rate spot-check ≥ 30 % (Open Food Facts coverage is Europe-heavy). True multi-photo flow (e.g. NF panel on one face, ingredients on the back) is a v2.x — current behaviour requires both pieces in one frame. Photo-of-prepared-dish recognition is out of plan indefinitely.

### 3.8.8 Ingredient substitution analysis *(SUBST-1, 2026-05-26; quality-guard + FPED ranking, 2026-05-28)*

Stylianou et al. (2021, *Nat Food* 2:616–627) quantify "small targeted dietary changes" as **explicit isocaloric replacements** — e.g. redirecting 10 % of calories from processed meat and beef toward fruit, vegetables, legumes and seafood — yielding ~+48 min healthy life per day and ~−33 % dietary carbon footprint at the population-modelling scale. Cardinaals et al. (2024, *Front Sustain Food Syst* 8:1304752) establish that nutrient-density (NRF) and disease-burden (HENI) indicators are largely uncorrelated and both weakly align with GWP, justifying multi-metric reporting rather than a single headline score. GBD 2017 Diet Collaborators (*Lancet* 2019;393:1958–1972, Discussion p. 1968) further require that any diet-shift counterfactual **name both sides of the exchange**, because cohort relative risks are energy-adjusted and an increase in one component implicitly substitutes for an unspecified decrease elsewhere. SUBST-1 closes the gap between those literature constructs and the platform's user-facing scoring stack: it is a **decision-support layer** that applies auditable ingredient replacements to a `{food_id, mass_g}[]` composition and re-runs the existing HEFI, HENI, HSR, FCS, environmental LCA, and dietary-pattern modules without duplicating nutrient arithmetic.

**Architecture.** The substitution stack ([`substitution_analyzer.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_analyzer.py), [`substitution_views.py`](backend/api/views/substitution_views.py)) accepts a CNF/WAFCT composition, optional `dish_name` and `cultural_context`, a scoring purpose (`general_health`, `lower_sodium`, `higher_fibre`, `sustainability`, …), and optional constraints (vegetarian, allergen exclusion, same functional role). Candidate replacements are drawn from four sources in priority order: (i) **four literature-anchored curated rules** aligned with Scenario S5 ([`substitution_rules.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_rules.py)): ground beef (CNF 2683) → lentils (3392); fluid cow's milk → fortified soy beverage (501528); sugar-sweetened cola (2920) → municipal water (2933); refined white bread (4066/3732) → whole-wheat bread (4067); (ii) embedding-based similar foods via the existing `CNFMatcher`; (iii) same-group nutrient discovery ([`substitution_discovery.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_discovery.py)); and (iv) for West African dishes, ingredient hints from the 195 mixed-dish recipes in WAFCT 2019 sheet 09 ([`wafct_recipes.py`](backend/api/services/wafct_recipes.py)). Each candidate replaces one ingredient slot at **unchanged mass**; indicators are recomputed through the production calculator paths already validated in §3.2–§3.8.6. Suggestions are ranked by purpose-weighted scores; for the top set a **six-metric scorecard** ([`substitution_scorecard.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_scorecard.py): HEFI, HENI minutes, HSR, FCS, ReCiPe H single-score per 100 kcal, dietary-pattern cosine) and a **Pareto non-dominated set** ([`substitution_pareto.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_pareto.py)) are reported.

**Quality pipeline (2026-05-28).** Embedding discovery alone proved insufficient: on a real Western recall day, the matcher surfaced anatomically wrong swaps (whole egg→yolk only; lean chicken→skin-on thigh) and nutritionally marginal noise (Greek yogurt, goat milk) that barely moved HEFI. We therefore added three post-retrieval filters before a suggestion reaches the UI:

1. **Culinary plausibility guards** ([`substitution_culinary.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_culinary.py)) — block egg↔yolk-only, lean poultry↔skin-on, dried↔fresh produce mismatches, and seasoning/oil false positives on low-fat dairy labels.
2. **Discovery quality gate** ([`substitution_quality.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_quality.py)) — require minimum ΔHEFI (≥ 0.2 pts) for matcher-only candidates; cap saturated-fat regressions; block plain→flavoured yogurt downgrades.
3. **FPED-aware gap-fill ranking** ([`substitution_fped_ranking.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_fped_ranking.py)) — when the day-level FPED profile shows shortfalls in fruits, vegetables, legumes, or whole grains, boost candidates that close those gaps rather than merely matching nutrient similarity.

Curated S5 rules receive a fixed rank bonus so literature-anchored swaps (e.g. milk→fortified soy) surface above marginal matcher alternatives. The improve-product page additionally renders an **FPED exposure panel** ([`FpedPanel.tsx`](frontend/src/components/shared/FpedPanel.tsx)) so users see food-group gaps before choosing swaps. Users trigger analysis manually ("Find swaps"); the panel does not auto-run on every composition edit.

**Endpoints and UX.** `POST /api/substitution/analyze/` returns ranked suggestions with optional scorecard and greedy multi-step reformulation plans (`reformulation_mode='greedy'`, up to four swaps). `POST /api/substitution/apply/` materialises one suggestion into a modified composition. `POST /api/substitution/batch/` scores multiple compositions in one call. The frontend surfaces substitution on `/improve-product`, as a second tab on `/scan-product` (after PKG-IMG-1 decomposition), and via per-row Swap actions in homemade composition forms — all sharing [`SubstitutionSuggestionsPanel.tsx`](frontend/src/components/shared/SubstitutionSuggestionsPanel.tsx).

**Downstream pipeline.** SUBST-1 sits downstream of §3.8 Feature 2 (recipe decomposer) and §3.8.7 (packaged-food ingredient decomposition): a decomposed label or homemade dish yields a CNF ingredient list that substitution then reformulates before re-scoring. HENI deltas are interpreted as **marginal effects of the stated swap** (Stylianou et al., 2021 Discussion; Cardinaals et al., 2024), not as diet-level population predictions; HEFI differences are **relative guideline-adherence shifts**, not validated health-outcome forecasts (Brassard et al., 2022b, Discussion p. 588).

**Validation.** Three harness layers ground the rollout ([`docs/substitution_manuscript_research.md`](docs/substitution_manuscript_research.md)): (Tier A) [`backend/_smoke_substitution_s5_panel.py`](backend/_smoke_substitution_s5_panel.py) — four canonical S5 single-ingredient swaps at fixed mass, full scorecard deltas → [`results/S5-subst/s5_delta_table.csv`](results/S5-subst/s5_delta_table.csv); (Tier B) [`backend/test_substitution_analyzer.py`](backend/test_substitution_analyzer.py) (**34/34 unit tests**, including culinary-guard and quality-gate regressions) + [`backend/_smoke_substitution_analyzer.py`](backend/_smoke_substitution_analyzer.py) (golden top-rule IDs for beef and white bread); (Tier D) [`backend/_smoke_substitution_s4_overlay.py`](backend/_smoke_substitution_s4_overlay.py) — deterministic application of all matching S5 rules onto each eligible day of the §4.6 S4-lite panel → [`results/S5-subst/s4_overlay.csv`](results/S5-subst/s4_overlay.csv). Tier A: **4/4 directional checks PASS** on 2026-05-26 (beef→legumes and cola→water win–win on HENI + environment; milk→soy documented as multi-metric trade-off). Tier D: **14/25** S4-lite days contain ≥1 S5-eligible ingredient; **14/14** improve HEFI after overlay; **9/14** win–win on HENI + ReCiPe environmental single-score.

**Known ranking gap (documented, not blocking §5.2).** On an **isolated beverage portion**, milk→soy can still show a negative composite rank score because HEFI-2019 dairy components do not map one-to-one onto plant beverages — even though HENI and environment improve. Manuscript counterfactual tables report **direct swap deltas** at fixed mass; whole-day context (where FPED ranking and curated-rule bonuses apply) is the intended decision-support setting.

### 3.8.9 Reconstruction-validated recipe decomposition *(DECOMP-VALID, 2026-05-28)*

Recipe decomposition (§3.8 Feature 2) sits upstream of every nutrition score, so its errors are inherited silently: if the ingredient list it returns does not reproduce the dish a user actually ate, then HEFI, HENI, HSR, FCS and the FPED food-group layer are all wrong in the same direction without any warning. The structural gates in §3.8 (mass closure, minimum ingredient count, confidence floor) confirm that a decomposition is internally coherent, but they cannot tell whether it is *correct*. To close that gap we built a ground-truth accuracy benchmark and then used what it found to repair the decomposer.

**Validation methodology.** We score each decomposition against four independent lenses, computed in one pass over a seed-stratified random sample of CNF composite foods (Mixed Dishes, Soups and Sauces, Fast Foods, Baked Products, Sweets, Snacks, Sausages and Luncheon Meats, and Babyfoods — the groups that genuinely require decomposing). The primary and strongest lens is **nutrient reconstruction**: we recompute the dish's per-100 g nutrients (energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, sugars, fibre, saturated fat, sodium) from its decomposed ingredients and compare them against the dish's *own measured CNF profile*, so the ground truth is the food's own laboratory values and the test covers every composite. The second lens is **food-group fidelity**, the cosine between the decomposition's FPED roll-up and the dish's FPED twin (§3.6). The third is the **structural gates** already described. The fourth is **agreement with USDA's authoritative recipe**: for composites bridged at confidence ≥ 0.7 we compare the decomposition's food-group roll-up against the real ingredient breakdown USDA publishes for the same food (FNDDS `input_food`, read through the FPID accessor). Each food earns a reproducible pass / borderline / flagged verdict, and we report per-group rates with 95 % bootstrap confidence intervals, reusing the matcher-benchmark template of §3.5 and §4.4.

**What the benchmark revealed.** Across 240 composite foods the decomposer reproduced food-group *shape* well (FPED cosine median 0.87) yet systematically misstated nutrient *magnitude*: only 16 % passed and 55 % were flagged, with a median macro error of 40 %. The dominant failure mode was a roughly two-fold calorie overcount on cooked and diluted dishes, because the model decomposed them into denser raw-ingredient forms and ignored the water the dish carries. A dehydrated chicken-noodle soup reconstituted with water (23 kcal/100 g) reconstructed at 218 kcal/100 g, nearly nine times too high; a cheese-and-pepperoni pizza (182 kcal/100 g) reconstructed at 365; baby-food purées roughly tripled. These errors flow straight into the disease-burden and diet-quality scores, which made composite dishes the least trustworthy part of the pipeline before this work. Only a reconstruction benchmark could have surfaced the problem, because the structural gates passed on most of these foods.

**The fix.** Three coordinated changes turn that finding into a repair. First, a **cooked-form-and-water rule** in the Stage-1 prompt instructs the model to choose as-served ingredient forms and to represent the dish's water explicitly; this is the only safeguard available for genuinely novel free-text dishes that have no catalogued analog. Second, **catalog preference**: when the dish name itself matches a CNF food that carries its own measured nutrients at high confidence (≥ 0.88 through the §3.8 matcher), the decomposer returns that food directly instead of inventing an ingredient split, which is both the most accurate path — the catalogued food's laboratory values are themselves the ground truth — and the cheapest, since it skips the language-model call entirely. Third, a **nutrient-reconstruction gate**: for weaker matches the decomposer still decomposes, but it overrides to the catalogued food whenever the reconstructed energy or macros diverge from a confident match (energy error above 25 % or mean macro error above 30 %) or the decomposition fails its structural gates outright. A `force_decompose` switch bypasses both catalog paths so that the validation harness and the golden regression tests keep measuring raw decomposition quality rather than the shortcut. Importantly, ingredient-level detail is not lost when catalog preference fires, because the FPID layer (§3.6) still resolves where each food group in the composite comes from, using USDA's recipe rather than a language model. Quantitative before-and-after results appear in §4.7.

### 3.9 Uncertainty quantification

The dominant European reference databases publish point estimates only: AGRIBALYSE provides a 1–5 Data Quality Rating but explicitly notes that quantitative standard deviations would require data not currently available (ADEME, 2024). We therefore build the uncertainty layer from primary-data variability. Each ReCiPe midpoint characterization factor (CF_m) and each upstream LCI factor is wrapped in a log-normal distribution with geometric standard deviation σ_g derived as follows: (a) for LCI factors covered by Poore & Nemecek (2018), σ_g is fitted from the published 10th-percentile-to-mean gap of Fig. 1 (p. 2) for each of the 40 product groups, with full distributions pulled from the deposited archive (doi.org/10.5287/bodleian:0z9MYbMyZ, Data S1) since acidification, eutrophication, and scarcity-weighted water values are bar-only in the printed figure; (b) for characterisation factors where ReCiPe publishes I/H/E factors, the I–E spread is used as a lower-bound on uncertainty; (c) all remaining factors fall back to ecoinvent pedigree-matrix defaults; (d) where neither is available we substitute the per-impact-category base GSD² priors of Stylianou et al. (2021) Suppl. Table 10 (p. 51), which range from 1.18 (terrestrial acidification, marine eutrophication) to 2.56 (ionising radiation) at midpoint, with the well-documented escalation to GSD² ≈ 6.39 for human non-cancer toxicity at endpoint. For GHGs specifically we apply the Stylianou et al. (2021) SI Eq. S8 scheme: CO₂ GSD² = 1 (reference), other GHGs GSD² = 1.4 (IPCC 2013), combined by the fossil-CO₂ share of total GWP. This approach is principled: Poore & Nemecek (2018) report up to 50-fold producer-level variation within a single product (p. 1), and the 90th-percentile-to-10th-percentile ratio exceeds 3× across all five indicators for major staples (p. 2).

For the **HENI track**, we propagate uncertainty over the published DRF 95 % CIs (Stylianou et al., 2021 SI Suppl. Table 3 p. 8) via the same Monte Carlo, with log-normal DRF distributions parameterised from the published lower/upper bounds. The relative DRF uncertainty is largest for red meat and milk (~58 % around the mean) and smallest for sodium and whole grains (~17 %), per the SI §S1.3 (p. 7).

We propagate uncertainty via Monte Carlo (N = 10 000) per meal, reporting medians and 5th–95th percentile intervals for each midpoint, each endpoint, the LCA single score, and HENI minutes. The iteration count follows the precision-vs-accuracy framework of Heijungs (2020, §§2.7–2.8, pp. 397–401): 10 000 iterations bound Monte Carlo sampling error well below the input-distribution uncertainty for the dominant food groups in Poore & Nemecek (n = 300–2 800 per group), but for the sparsest groups (n < 500) N_run exceeds Heijungs' recommended cap; this trade-off is flagged in §7.5 and the reported percentile intervals at sparse-group resolution should be read as conservative envelopes on producer-variability draws rather than as statistically rigorous CIs. Sobol first-order and total-order indices are computed (SALib; Saltelli, 2008) to attribute output variance to factor groups (energy/feed; manure & enteric; land-use change; transport & retail; characterization to endpoint; normalization; per-DRF nutritional uncertainty). The Egalitarian–Hierarchist climate-CF gap of ~14× at endpoint (e.g. 1.25 × 10⁻⁵ vs 9.28 × 10⁻⁷ DALY kg⁻¹ CO₂; workbook-loaded values per §3.9) is the largest single uncertainty driver and — as of the 2026-05-22 multi-perspective refactor — is now a runtime parameter rather than a code edit: setting `perspective='E'` in the LCA constructor swaps the entire midpoint-to-endpoint factor stack to the Egalitarian column of the RIVM workbook, with no recomputation required, and the same applies to `perspective='I'`. We report all three perspectives as a sensitivity panel rather than baking one in. We additionally cross-check the Monte Carlo bounds against the analytical Taylor-series propagation of Hong et al. (2010), the closed-form alternative used by Stylianou et al. (2021) SI §S3.5 — agreement within ±10 % is the success criterion (Scenario S3).

### 3.10 Country and perspective parameterisation

ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 publishes country-specific characterisation factors for only five impact categories — fine particulate matter formation (66 source regions), photochemical ozone formation (70), terrestrial acidification (224 country rows), freshwater eutrophication (159), and water consumption (288) (Huijbregts et al., 2017, Abstract and §4.2, p. 145). Other categories (climate change, ionising radiation, ozone depletion, land use, the toxicities and resource scarcity) are deliberately reported as global values because their underlying models either operate at the planetary level (e.g. radiative forcing) or do not yet have defensible spatially-explicit data (e.g. land use; RIVM 2017 Ch. 12 / Table 1.1 p. 18). The country workbook accompanying the v1.1 release (`ReCiPe2016_country factors_v1.1_20171221.xlsx`) is the authoritative source.

Our pipeline now ingests all three official RIVM workbooks (`ReCiPe2016_CFs_v1.1_20180117.xlsx` for midpoint-to-endpoint conversion factors at all three cultural perspectives, `Normalization scores ReCiPe2016v1.1_20190514.xlsx` for World 2010 per-person normalisation scores at midpoint and endpoint, and the country factors workbook above) via a deterministic ETL into checksummed JSON packs that the runtime loader (`environmental_impact_model.src.methodology_factors.MethodologyFactorPack`) validates against the recorded SHA-256 at load. Runtime code holds no hard-coded LCA constants; every factor traces back to a workbook cell with full provenance (extraction timestamp, ETL git rev, source file SHA-256 — all surfaced in the API's `methodology_provenance` block).

This refactor introduces three runtime parameters, exposed both through the LCA constructor and the API surface:

- **`perspective ∈ {I, H, E}`** (default **H**, RIVM convention) — chooses the cultural perspective for every midpoint-to-endpoint conversion factor and every normalisation score. Switching to Egalitarian inflates the Human Health endpoint for a high-GW meal by ~13× because the GW-to-DALY factor jumps from 9.28 × 10⁻⁷ (H) to 1.25 × 10⁻⁵ (E) per the workbook. This is the largest single uncertainty driver (§3.8) and is now runnable as a parameter, not a code edit.
- **`country`** (ISO-3, default **None** = world-average) — when set in conjunction with `consumer_perspective='national'`, the loader substitutes the workbook's country-specific endpoint CFs for the three water-consumption pathways (`water_use_human`, `water_use_ecosystem_terrestrial`, `water_use_ecosystem_freshwater`); a per-pathway audit dict (`endpoint_factor_sources`) records which factor source was used. The country workbook supplies CFs for 246 ISO-3 codes after name normalisation. For categories with per-country data at midpoint but not at our consumed-midpoint resolution (acidification, freshwater eutrophication: substance-level CFs that require a per-substance LCI inventory to apply), the country data is shipped in the JSON pack for forward use under TODO-CODE-LCA-2 but not currently substituted at runtime. Land use and the toxicities have no country-specific workbook data and remain on world-average factors.
- **`consumer_perspective ∈ {global, national}`** (default **global**) — explicit toggle that controls whether country-specific CFs are substituted at all. The default reflects that most CNF food products have multi-country supply chains for which world-average factors are more defensible than any single national lens; `national` is the appropriate choice for in-country produced and consumed analyses (e.g. a Canadian meal of Canadian-grown produce against Canadian-context indicators).

The previously-shipped midpoint-level "Canadian regional factors" — a set of seventeen hand-tuned multipliers (e.g. water 0.65, land 0.78) for which we could not produce LCA-literature provenance — were retired in this same revision. The country-aware logic now lives only at the endpoint conversion step, where the workbook authoritatively supports it. We report both the regionalised (`consumer_perspective='national'`) and unadjusted (`'global'`) runs in §5.3 (Scenario S6) following the same protocol as Dekker et al. (2019, §3.3, pp. 5–6).

**Interaction with the §3.5 matcher.** The country-aware substitution applies to *endpoint factors*, not midpoint values; the matcher continues to overlay Agribalyse 3.2 per-food midpoints over the cnf_integrator group defaults exactly as before, and the matched midpoints then flow through whichever endpoint factor (world-average or country-specific) the configuration selects. There is no longer any midpoint-level multiplier that double-bakes geography, so the previous matched-vs-unmatched scaling suppression rule is no longer needed.

**Normalisation.** Per-AoP endpoint normalisation scores (Human Health: 0.0240 DALY person⁻¹ yr⁻¹; Ecosystems: 8.56 × 10⁻⁴ species·yr person⁻¹ yr⁻¹; Resources: 2.77 × 10⁴ USD₂₀₁₃ person⁻¹ yr⁻¹ for Hierarchist) are loaded directly from the published workbook rather than transcribed; earlier drafts that referred to a "2024 RIVM normalisation revision" have been retired as the underlying RIVM artefact post-dating October 2017 v1.1 was not retrievable. We additionally expose per-category midpoint normalisation (`calculate_normalized_midpoints`) so reviewers can read individual contributions as fractions of an average global citizen's annual midpoint footprint (e.g. a 100 g beef serving's Global warming impact expressed as a fraction of 7990 kg CO₂-eq person⁻¹ yr⁻¹).

**Functional-unit basis (Tier α).** The pipeline computes four functional-unit bases simultaneously for every meal: `per_serving` (raw absolute meal impact at the user-submitted quantity), `per_100g_product` (mass-normalised), `per_100_kcal` (caloric-density-fair, the published P&N Panel C basis), and `per_100g_protein` (protein-source-fair, the P&N Panel A basis). Aggregation happens once in absolute mass space (the meal's raw kg CO₂-eq / m²·yr / m³) and is then scaled into each basis by the corresponding denominator; the chosen `basis` request parameter selects which basis surfaces as the "headline" output, but the full multi-basis dict is always returned under `impacts_by_basis`. This addresses a recurring critique that "per 100 g product" alone is a weak universal basis when water fraction and energy density differ across foods (raw cucumber ≈ 96 % water, dry pasta ≈ 4 % water, but both have meaningful per-100-kcal impacts). The default basis remains `per_100_kcal` for backward compatibility with prior figures. Single-score normalisation and per-category person-year-equivalent reporting use the `per_serving` (raw) basis so the per-person-year reference is dimensionally consistent — replacing an earlier draft that passed per-100-kcal-scaled values into a per-person-year denominator and produced a dimensionally mixed score.

**Per-food-group factor derivation.** The fallback per-CNF-food-group midpoint centrals consumed when the §3.5 matcher returns no high-confidence match are derived from Poore & Nemecek (2018) Fig. 1 panel values (per 100 g protein in Panel A; per 1 L in Panel B; per 1 000 kcal in Panel C; per 1 kg in Panels E–F) and Mekonnen & Hoekstra (2011/2012) blue-water-only consumptive footprints. Because P&N's functional units differ by panel while the pipeline expresses everything per 100 g product, the conversion requires applying a panel-specific factor (protein fraction, density, or kcal density). As of the 2026-05-22 refactor, these conversion constants are explicit module-scope dicts (`_PN_PANEL_CENTRALS`, `_DERIVATION_CONSTANTS`) and the per-group centrals are computed deterministically by `_derive_group_centrals()` rather than hand-typed alongside inline comments. Three substantive corrections landed in the same revision: (i) Beef Products GHG was 6.7 kg CO₂-eq / 100 g (an inadvertent beef-herd + dairy-herd blend); the consistent beef-herd-only derivation gives 50 × 0.20 = **10.0**; Land had always used beef-herd only at 32.8. (ii) Dairy and Egg Products Land was 9.0 m²·yr / 100 g (cheese-only, 41 × 0.22); the arithmetic blend of cheese + milk + egg components gives **3.52**, removing a 2.5× over-statement of dairy/egg land impact for the typically milk-dominant Canadian dietary pattern. (iii) Cereals, Grains and Pasta used 350 kcal/100 g (a dry-grain assumption) to apply P&N Panel C; the CNF stores as-consumed entries that mix cooked rice (~130), cooked pasta (~158), bread (~265), and dry flour (~350), with a representative mid of **200 kcal/100 g** — yielding GHG = 0.18 and Land = 0.28 (was 0.32 / 0.49). An independent five-test panel-anchored validator (`CnfIntegratorPanelDerivationTests` in [`backend/environmental_impact_model/tests/test_lca_v1_trim.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/tests/test_lca_v1_trim.py)) recomputes every group's central from the raw panel values plus the conversion constants and asserts it matches the shipped value; future edits to either side without the other fail the test. Reviewers can therefore argue with specific protein-fraction or kcal-density assumptions rather than with opaque per-group numbers.

### 3.11 Reproducibility

All factors, prompts, retrieval indices, benchmark labels, notebooks and figures are released in the `paper_v1` branch of the repository under Apache 2.0. Results are reproducible from CNF 2015 with one command (`make figures`).

**Build note (Rust core).** The performance-critical numerical kernels for HSR, FCS and HENI compile to a PyO3-bound native extension (`backend/rust_core/`). Any change to `backend/rust_core/src/` requires rebuilding the installed `.pyd`/`.so` with `cd backend/rust_core && maturin develop --release`; the Python import path resolves to the compiled binary, not the live `.rs` source, so a stale binary will silently return values from the previous compilation. We document this explicitly because a stale `rust_core.cp313-win_amd64.pyd` (built 2026-04-15, predating the 2026-05-21 HENI-CODE-1 sign-convention refactor) caused 5/15 HENI unit tests to fail intermittently during a 2026-05-23 audit until the rebuild was run; the build-staleness incident is logged in [`code_action_items.md`](code_action_items.md) HENI-CODE-1.y as a maintainer-facing reproducibility note.

The LCA factor data is layered specifically for auditability. The three official RIVM workbooks (`ReCiPe2016_CFs_v1.1_20180117.xlsx`, `Normalization scores ReCiPe2016v1.1_20190514.xlsx`, `ReCiPe2016_country factors_v1.1_20171221.xlsx`) ship in the repository alongside a deterministic ETL ([`build_recipe2016_factor_packs.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/etl/build_recipe2016_factor_packs.py)) that converts them into four checksummed JSON packs and an ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 name normalisation map (246 countries after spelling reconciliation). The runtime loader ([`methodology_factors.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/methodology_factors.py)) validates each pack's SHA-256 against the recorded value in `recipe2016_factor_packs_meta.json` at module load and logs a one-line version string of the form `recipe2016:v1.1:endpoint_sha=<12char>:perspectives=3:countries=246`. This same string is surfaced in every API response under `metadata.methodology_pack`, so any figure or table in the paper can be traced back to the exact pack version that produced it. The test suite — 83 tests across [`environmental_impact_model/tests/`](backend/environmental_impact_model/tests/) — pins (i) every workbook value the loader exposes, (ii) the per-CNF-food-group factor derivation chain from raw P&N panel values + documented conversion constants, (iii) the country-aware endpoint CF substitution rules, (iv) the v1 trim to {Global warming, Land use, Water consumption} as the consumed midpoint vector, and (v) the parity invariants under default settings (`country=None`, `perspective='H'`, `consumer_perspective='global'`). Rebuilding the JSON packs from the workbooks with `python -m environmental_impact_model.etl.build_recipe2016_factor_packs` reproduces the same SHA-256s deterministically across platforms.

### 4.4 Scenario S7 — LLM food-to-LCA matcher accuracy

**Methodology.** A reproducible benchmark harness ([`backend/_smoke_matcher_benchmark.py`](backend/_smoke_matcher_benchmark.py)) runs the live §3.5 matcher (OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` retrieval + the configured ranking LLM — production default `gpt-4.1-mini` since the 2026-05-22 calibration upgrade; the original 184-food benchmark result tabulated below was obtained under `gpt-4o-mini` and is preserved here as the pre-upgrade baseline) against a stratified random sample of 184 CNF foods (8 per CNF FoodGroup × 23 groups; seeded for reproducibility). For each match, four automated quality heuristics are applied: (1) **group consistency** — matched Agribalyse `agribalyse_group` is in the expected set for the CNF FoodGroup (e.g. CNF "Beef Products" must match `viandes, œufs, poissons`, never `produits sucrés`); (2) **magnitude plausibility** — matched per-100 g GW within ±3× of the cnf_integrator group-default for the CNF group (within-group P&N variance is ~50×, so ±3× of the GROUP MEAN catches only catastrophic cross-domain mismatches); (3) **token overlap** — after the §3.5 state-canonicalisation pre-filter, the matched LCI name (English + French combined) shares ≥1 content token (≥4 chars, stoplist-filtered) with the canonicalised CNF description; (4) **confidence band** — `clean` ≥ 0.85, `borderline` 0.60–0.85, `low` < 0.60. A match is `clean` if all four pass AND confidence ≥ 0.85; `borderline` if all four pass at lower confidence; `flagged` if any of #1–3 fails OR matched=False. A reviewer then spot-checks the `flagged` rows (≈ 60–70 per 184-food run) using the auto-generated [`matcher_benchmark_flagged_for_review.md`](backend/environmental_impact_model/data/matcher_benchmark_flagged_for_review.md) markdown report. Full per-food results — including JSON fields for `reviewer_verdict` + `reviewer_notes` — are persisted in [`data/matcher_benchmark_<git-rev>_<utc>.json`](backend/environmental_impact_model/data/) (git-rev-stamped for reproducibility). Cost: $0.026 per 184-food run (≤ $0.10 budget); median latency 1.6 s per food.

**Pre-upgrade baseline result** (184-food stratified sample, git rev `16a5ca7`, `gpt-4o-mini`, 2026-05-22): **28 % `clean` / 35 % `borderline` / 37 % `flagged`** under the automated heuristics. The confidence calibration was nominally meaningful (`≥ 0.85` band: 26 % flagged; `0.60–0.85` band: 43 % flagged) but the per-food confidence distribution was concentrated at the top: ≈ 77 % of matches reported confidence ≥ 0.85, with the model rarely admitting genuine uncertainty (an artefact of the same `gpt-4o-mini` self-confidence-anchoring documented in §3.5).

**Post-upgrade result** (184-food stratified sample, git rev `de2de57`, `gpt-4.1-mini` + rewritten prompts per Tian et al. 2023, 2026-05-22, $0.026 cost / 2.2 s median latency unchanged): **14 % `clean` / 45 % `borderline` / 42 % `flagged`** under the same automated heuristics. The headline `clean` rate dropped by 14 pp, but **this is the calibration improvement, not a regression**: the same stratified panel produces *more accurate* matches at the prices where the model claims high confidence. Across the three confidence bands:

| Band | n (pre) | flagged-rate (pre) | n (post) | flagged-rate (post) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **≥ 0.85** | 142 | 26 % | **33** | **24 %** |
| **0.60–0.85** | 38 | 43 % | **125** | **34 %** |
| **< 0.60** | 4 | (n too small) | **26** | **100 %** |

The new confidence distribution uses the full 0.2–1.0 range (counts at conf ≈ 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8 / 0.9 / 1.0: 24 / 40 / 104 / 4 / 10) where the pre-upgrade run collapsed most decisions into the ≥ 0.85 bucket. Three interpretation points:

1. **The high-confidence band shrank by 78 % but stayed reliable** (24 % flagged rate, within sampling noise of the prior 26 %). When `gpt-4.1-mini` reports ≥ 0.85 it is meaningfully claiming "an LCA expert would call this match correct"; the pre-upgrade model was reporting that level on many stretched near-misses.

2. **The borderline band improved meaningfully**, from 43 % → 34 % flagged. The matches the pre-upgrade model put into the ≥ 0.85 bucket but that an expert would have downgraded now land in 0.60–0.85 where they belong; the heuristics' flag rate on the larger borderline set is correspondingly lower because it contains both genuine close-but-not-exact matches *and* the previously-overconfident ones (which still pass the structural heuristics but no longer mis-claim certainty).

3. **The `< 0.60` band is now operational, with 26 honest admissions** (all flagged by design, since the band's confidence-band check is the flag trigger). Examples drawn from the artefact include "Dessert, pudding, vanilla, dry mix → Sugar, vanilla flavoured" (conf 0.4), "Tea, instant, decaffeinated → Tea, brewed, without sugar" (conf 0.4), "Beef jerky → Dry sausage" (conf 0.4). These are matches the pre-upgrade model returned at ≥ 0.6 confidence as silent near-misses; the post-upgrade model surfaces them as low-confidence and they route to the group-default fallback rather than overwriting it. This is the verbalised-confidence calibration improvement (Tian et al., 2023) operationalised end-to-end.

**Architectural impact.** Under `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD = 0.85`, only 33 / 184 (18 %) of post-upgrade matches stay on the direct-match path; the remaining 151 borderline + low-confidence matches now route either to the Tier γ recipe decomposer (for composite CNF groups) or to the cnf_integrator group-default fallback. Under the pre-upgrade model the same threshold left 142 / 184 (77 %) on the direct path — a 4× over-acceptance rate driven by the same model self-confidence-anchoring artefact §3.5 documents. The architectural promise of the 0.85 threshold and the Tier γ trigger predicate (`RecipeDecomposer.should_decompose`) is therefore only fully operational under the post-upgrade model.

**Per-group flagged-rate distribution** (post-upgrade run, descending; sample n = 8 per group; 95 % percentile-bootstrap CIs from 1000 resamples in brackets): Nuts and Seeds 100 % [100, 100], Legumes 88 % [63, 100], Sweets 88 % [63, 100], Finfish / Fats and Oils / Breakfast cereals 63 % [25, 88-100], Dairy and Egg / Poultry / Fruits / Beef / Vegetables 50 % [13, 75-88], Mixed Dishes 38 % [13, 75], Spices / Cereals-Grains-Pasta / Snacks / Lamb-Veal-Game / Baked Products / Pork 25 % [0, 50-63], Beverages / Fast Foods / Babyfoods / Sausages / Soups 13 % [0, 38]. **The CIs are wide because n = 8 per group is statistically thin** — most rates land within a ±25–40 pp window by sampling alone, so per-group point estimates should be treated as ordinal evidence (Nuts/Sweets/Legumes are clearly the hardest tier; Beverages/Babyfoods/Soups are clearly the easiest tier) rather than as precise figures. Only Nuts and Seeds at 100 % flagged has a degenerate CI (all 8 panel members flagged → no variance under bootstrap). A reviewer planning to make subgroup statistical claims should re-run with `--sample-size 460` (n = 20 per group, tightening CI half-width to ±13 pp).

**Latency tail.** Per-food latency on the post-upgrade run: p50 = 2.23 s, p75 = 5.74 s, **p90 = 7.70 s, p95 = 18.71 s, p99 = 53.87 s**, max = 61.30 s, mean = 5.01 s. The mean is more than 2× the median, dominated by a long tail of ≈ 5 % of foods where the LLM ranker takes 20 s+ (likely concatenated retries on slow OpenAI responses; not reproducible across runs). Production UX should size against p95 (≈ 19 s) rather than mean or median; batch / async paths should expect occasional p99 outliers near 1 min.

**Failure taxonomy (post-upgrade n = 77 flagged rows).** A read of the auto-generated `matcher_benchmark_flagged_for_review.md` classifies flagged matches into five recurring failure modes (mutually-exclusive heuristic-derived primary category; counts approximate, ordered by frequency):

1. **Vocabulary-mismatch / no canonical Agribalyse entry** (≈ 32 / 77, dominant in Sweets, Fats and Oils, Beverages). The CNF food has no near-counterpart in the v32 catalogue's French-curated vocabulary: dry baking mixes ("Vanilla pudding dry mix" → "Sugar, vanilla flavoured", conf 0.4), Anglo-style salad dressings ("Italian dressing, commercial" → "Olive oil, extra virgin", conf 0.4), instant beverages ("Tea, instant, decaffeinated, powder" → "Tea, brewed, without sugar", conf 0.4), confectionery sub-forms ("Frozen ice pop" → "Hard candy and lollipop", conf 0.2). These are correctly reported at < 0.60 confidence and route to the group default — the matcher is honestly admitting the candidate vocabulary lacks the right entry, which is the calibrated behaviour we want.

2. **Right-food-wrong-processing-form / magnitude-artefact** (≈ 18 / 77, dominant in Nuts and Seeds — accounts for the 100 % flagged rate). The matcher picks the correct base commodity but Agribalyse does not separately catalogue the processed form, so the magnitude check fires on the energy-density uplift. Examples: peanut flour (defatted) → peanut (whole), ratio 7.74×; almonds toasted unblanched → almonds peeled/unpeeled, 4.38×; walnuts glazed → walnut dried husked, 6.85×; pecans oil-roasted salted → pecan salted, 6.10×; vegetarian patty → plant-based patty, 3.11×. **The flag is not a defect**: processing (roasting / drying / defatting) concentrates kcal density and the corresponding per-100 g LCA values, so the post-upgrade matcher correctly distinguishes whole nut from processed nut even when the LCI vocabulary collapses them. The Tier δ per-commodity LCI re-scoring (TODO-CODE-LCA-2) would resolve this systematically by exposing process variants as distinct LCI rows.

3. **Right-group-wrong-species** (≈ 11 / 77, dominant in Finfish, Poultry, Lamb-Veal-Game). Agribalyse lacks the specific species and the matcher reaches for the closest taxonomically-related row: tilefish → Atlantic halibut (conf 0.6); emu rump → duck leg (conf 0.6); whale → game pâté (conf 0.4); goldeye → golden redfish (conf 0.4); pike liver → Atlantic herring lean raw (conf 0.4); veal pancreas → veal kidney sautéed (conf 0.4); pork lungs → pork brain braised (conf 0.4); beet greens → Swiss chard cooked (conf 0.6). These are honest "closest available" stretches; the < 0.60 ones fall through to the group default, the 0.60–0.75 ones surface as borderline for review.

4. **Right-but-wrong-state (cooked vs raw asymmetry)** (≈ 9 / 77, dominant in Beef Products and Pork Products). Agribalyse v32 catalogues raw beef cuts but not always their cooked counterparts; the matcher picks the raw form and the magnitude ratio is suppressed by ~3× because the cooked CNF entry concentrates mass: beef rib roast cooked → beef rib raw, ratio 0.29×; beef rump roast broiled → beef rump steak raw, 0.29×; beef ground patty pan-fried → burger 15% raw, 0.17×; pork ham roasted → cooked ham, 0.50×; chicken meat-and-skin raw → chicken roasted (the only direction this CNF entry exists in Agribalyse), conf 0.4. The magnitude flag is technically a calibration artefact (the cooked-CNF / raw-Agribalyse pairing's "true" LCA distance is small, the absolute per-100 g GW differs because of post-cooking water loss).

5. **Wildcard / cross-category reach** (≈ 7 / 77, dominant in composite groups). When no candidate fits, the matcher accepts a structurally-distant proxy and reports very low confidence: pumpkin pie → pumpkin canned drained (conf 0.4); chicken gravy canned → chicken with curry+coconut sauce (conf 0.4); rice cake → salty crackers (conf 0.4); pickled cucumber dill → sauerkraut with garnish (conf 0.4); candied ginger → "Candies, all types" (conf 0.4). The post-upgrade calibration handles these correctly — confidence drops to ≤ 0.40 and the group default fallback fires.

**Implication for the architecture.** Failure modes 1 and 5 (≈ 39 / 77, > 50 % of flagged rows) are correctly drained by the post-upgrade calibration alone — these were silent near-misses pre-upgrade and are now honest admissions. Failure modes 2 and 4 (≈ 27 / 77) are LCA-relevant distinctions that the matcher correctly identifies via the magnitude check and that **require Agribalyse vocabulary expansion** (resolved by Tier δ / TODO-CODE-LCA-2, not by further matcher tuning). Failure mode 3 (≈ 11 / 77) is genuinely an embedding-recall limit — for niche species the candidate pool simply does not contain the right answer.

The investigation harness ([`backend/_analyze_decomposer_agreement.py`](backend/_analyze_decomposer_agreement.py)) and the markdown summary ([`backend/environmental_impact_model/data/decomposer_agreement_analysis.md`](backend/environmental_impact_model/data/decomposer_agreement_analysis.md)) ship with the artefact for reproducibility.

**Flag-rule symmetry caveat.** The four heuristics are not exactly equal-stringency across CNF groups. (a) The **group-consistency check** is a wildcard pass (always TRUE) for Snacks and Spices and Herbs and accepts four Agribalyse groups for Mixed Dishes, so those groups can only fail on magnitude or token overlap; in contrast Beverages, Babyfoods, Nuts and Seeds, Vegetables, and Fats and Oils accept exactly one Agribalyse group. The 13 % flagged rate on Snacks reflects this looseness in part, but the 13 % rate on Beverages, Babyfoods, Soups, Sausages and Fast Foods does NOT (those have strict or two-group consistency rules and just have good Agribalyse coverage). (b) The **magnitude check** uses the same ±3× rule against each group's cnf_integrator default GW, but the absolute sensitivity differs by group: groups with low defaults (Nuts 0.06, Legumes 0.054 kg CO₂-eq / 100 g) flag a higher fraction because processed forms blow past the multiplicative window, even though the absolute kg CO₂-eq difference is small. This is the *intended* calibration: within-group P&N variance is ~50× per Poore & Nemecek (2018), and the ±3× of the GROUP MEAN catches processing-energy uplift that the matcher needs to surface. (c) The **token-overlap check** uses an identical stoplist across all groups (a single 21-word list of state/preparation modifiers), so it is structurally symmetric. We document this caveat for reviewers so the per-group rates are not over-interpreted as pure matcher quality differences.

**Per-group threshold calibration (design proposal, not yet shipped).** The current production code uses a single `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD = 0.85` and the existing decomposer trigger (`should_decompose`) routes the 0.60–0.85 band on composite CNF groups to Tier γ. The post-upgrade benchmark suggests this threshold could be calibrated **per CNF FoodGroup**, because the matcher's reliability at a given verbalised-confidence level varies systematically with the group's flagged rate: at conf ≥ 0.85, Nuts and Seeds is still 100 % flagged (the magnitude check is hard to satisfy), so the threshold could be raised to 0.95 for that group (or the magnitude window relaxed when the LCI's `agribalyse_category` denotes a processed form); for Beverages and Babyfoods, where vocabulary coverage is dense and the 0.60–0.85 band's flagged rate is well under the panel average, the threshold could be relaxed to 0.75. A proposed table, **based on the post-upgrade n = 184 panel** and intended as a starting point for v2 (none of these are shipped yet):

| CNF FoodGroup | Current threshold | Proposed | Reason |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Nuts and Seeds | 0.85 | **0.95** | 100 % flagged at every band; magnitude-uplift dominates; raise to make any acceptance a strict signal |
| Legumes and Legume Products | 0.85 | **0.90** | 88 % flagged; similar magnitude-uplift pattern |
| Sweets | 0.85 | **0.90** | 88 % flagged; vocabulary-mismatch dominant |
| Beef Products, Pork Products, Lamb-Veal-Game | 0.85 | **0.85** (unchanged) | Within-group variance is real; current threshold balances false-accept vs. false-reject |
| Beverages, Babyfoods, Soups-Sauces-Gravies | 0.85 | **0.75** | Low flagged rate at borderline; relax to capture more direct matches |
| Mixed Dishes, Fast Foods, Baked Products | 0.85 | **0.85** (unchanged) | Composite groups; threshold + Tier γ trigger combination already operational |
| All others | 0.85 | **0.85** (unchanged) | No benchmark evidence for adjustment |

Implementing this requires a new `_GROUP_HIGH_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLDS` constant in [`recipe_decomposer.py`](backend/environmental_impact_model/src/recipe_decomposer.py) and a per-group lookup in `should_decompose`; validation requires re-running the benchmark under the per-group thresholds and checking that the flagged rate at the new bands stays below the global rate. This is a v2 deliverable; the post-upgrade single-threshold path is sufficient for the current paper.

**Interpretation.** This is a **matcher behavioural benchmark** — it measures whether the LLM's chosen Agribalyse entry is structurally plausible (right group, right magnitude, right text overlap) and whether the model's verbalised confidence is well-calibrated against those structural checks — not a true accuracy benchmark of the underlying LCA value. The latter requires per-CNF expert LCA labels which are part of the v2 scope blocked on TODO-CODE-LCA-2 (licensed AGRIBALYSE-LCI re-scoring under ReCiPe characterisation factors). The Tier γ recipe decomposer (§3.5) and the borderline-confidence trigger refinement (`HIGH_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD = 0.85`, `RecipeDecomposer.should_decompose`) are empirically motivated by both the pre- and post-upgrade benchmarks — under the pre-upgrade model the decomposer never activated in production because the matcher reported `matched=True` at ≥ 0.85 confidence on stretched LCA-distant near-misses (Bannock → "Biscuit, extruded and grilled, fruits filling" at 0.65; Tourtière → "Riesling wine and pork pie" at 0.60). Under the post-upgrade model the same borderline-confidence matches on composite CNF groups now correctly land in the 0.60–0.85 band and route to ingredient-level decomposition instead.

### 4.6 S4-lite cross-indicator panel — methodology (Scenario S4 fallback)

**Why this panel exists.** Full Scenario S4 (§5.1) targets 100 CCHS-Nutrition 24-h-recall medoids via Statistics Canada RDC access. S4-lite is the documented synthetic fallback ([`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md) §S4): a hand-curated set of full-day diets scored across all platform indicators to populate the Spearman correlation matrix and §6.2 trade-off exemplars before RDC data are available.

**Panel construction (n = 25 days).** Each day is a fixed list of `{CNF FoodID, mass_g}` pairs — no LLM recipe decomposition, no packaged-food label inference. Days were assembled to span the nutritional and pattern spectrum:

- **3 days** from the HEFI canonical-diet smoke harness ([`_smoke_hefi_canonical_diets.py`](backend/_smoke_hefi_canonical_diets.py)): deep-fried anti-pattern (D01), mixed-balanced representative (D02), CFG-2019-aligned ideal (D03).
- **10 days** from the DIET-PATTERN-1 G3 reference-day set ([`_smoke_dietary_pattern.py`](backend/_smoke_dietary_pattern.py)): Mediterranean, vegetarian, Western BBQ, WAFCT West African staple, vegan, DASH, CFG healthy plate, EAT-Lancet, Italian Mediterranean, fast-food Western (D04–D13).
- **12 days** composed for S4-lite to cover active-adult, legume-forward, beef-heavy, dairy-heavy, low-calorie, processed-noodle, sedentary, youth lunchbox, and elderly-light patterns (D14–D25), using the same verified CNF FoodIDs as the §4.5 six-meal panel and recall-pattern proxies where applicable.

The full day list, masses, and per-day scores are in [`results/S4-lite/meals_panel.csv`](results/S4-lite/meals_panel.csv).

**Scoring protocol.** All indicators are computed through the production REST API ([`_smoke_s4_lite_panel.py`](backend/_smoke_s4_lite_panel.py)) with default platform configuration (individual audience; no LLM LCA matcher). For each day:

| Indicator | Endpoint | Reported scalar | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|-------|
| HEFI-2019 | `POST /api/hefi/calculate/` | `total_score` (0–80) | Full-day aggregate over all foods in the list |
| HENI | `POST /api/heni/calculate/` | `health_impact_minutes` | Signed minutes of healthy life (positive = beneficial); FPED-grounded composition per §3.6 |
| HSR | `POST /api/hsr/calculate/` | energy-weighted per-product ★ | When `len(foods) > 1`, `from_recall24h=true` → per-food HSRAC v9 category at recall portion size, then energy-weighted mean (`per_food_summary.energy_weighted_avg`; SCORECARD-1). Combined-meal HSR is omitted for multi-food days. |
| FCS-10 | `POST /api/fcs/calculate/` | `fcs` (1–100 Mozaffarian scale) | Combined-meal score over the full food list |
| Global warming | `POST /api/environmental-impact/` | `all_impacts['Global warming']` | ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 Hierarchist; **functional unit = per 100 kcal**; cnf_integrator group-default LCA (`enable_lca_matcher=false`) |

Dietary-pattern top-1 labels were also computed (`POST /api/dietary-pattern/classify/`) for narrative context but are excluded from the Spearman matrix below.

**Correlation analysis.** Pairwise **Spearman rank correlations (ρ)** were computed on the **25 day-level scores** — i.e. each day contributes one rank per indicator, and ρ measures whether days that rank high on indicator A also rank high on indicator B. This extends the §4.5 six-*meal* coherence check to a broader day-level panel and adds FCS and environmental GW. Implementation: average-rank tie-breaking, identical to [`_smoke_nutrition_cross_system.py`](backend/_smoke_nutrition_cross_system.py). No p-values are reported at n = 25; correlations are descriptive rank-order diagnostics, not inferential tests against a national sample.

**Reproducibility.** `cd backend && python _smoke_s4_lite_panel.py` (~20 s on a warm CNF integrator). Artefacts: [`backend/_smoke_s4_lite_panel_results.json`](backend/_smoke_s4_lite_panel_results.json), [`results/S4-lite/spearman_matrix.csv`](results/S4-lite/spearman_matrix.csv).

**Limitations.** The panel is **not CCHS-representative** — it is a purposive synthetic sample designed for cross-indicator rank diagnostics and trade-off illustration, not for reproducing Brassard et al. (2022b) national HEFI means (§5.1 benchmarks). HSR is per-product weighted rather than a daily HSR score (HSRAC v9 has no validated day-level aggregate). Environmental scores use group-default LCA factors, not Agribalyse-matched per-food factors (§3.5 matcher disabled for reproducibility and cost). GW is one ReCiPe midpoint category; monetised externalities and endpoint scores are computed but not included in the correlation matrix reported in §6.2.

**Headline results (2026-05-26 run).** Nutrition indicators correlate positively at the day level: HEFI–HENI ρ = **+0.837**, HEFI–FCS +0.797, HSR–FCS +0.763, HEFI–HSR +0.646. Global-warming intensity (per 100 kcal) correlates negatively with nutrition: HEFI–GW ρ = **−0.422**, HENI–GW −0.585, FCS–GW −0.512. Interpretation and worked trade-off examples: §6.2.

### 4.7 Recipe-decomposition fidelity and the catalog-preference fix *(DECOMP-VALID, 2026-05-28)*

We ran the four-lens benchmark of §3.8.9 on the same 240-food stratified sample before and after the fix, and in two modes: the full production pipeline (catalog preference active) and a `force_decompose` mode that isolates the raw language-model decomposition. Table 4.7 reports the headline.

| Metric (240 composite foods) | Before fix | Force-decompose (prompt only) | Full pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nutrient pass-rate | 16 % | 21 % | **99 %** |
| Flagged for nutrient error | 55 % | 51 % | **0 %** |
| Median macro error | 0.40 | 0.36 | **0.00** |
| Catalog-hit rate | — | 0 % | **97 %** |

In production the result is decisive. Catalog preference resolves 97 % of composite dishes directly to their measured CNF food, so the dish a user names is scored from its own laboratory values and none are flagged for nutrient error; the composite-dish accuracy problem is, in effect, eliminated for any dish that already exists in the food table. The cooked-form prompt rule gives the raw decomposition path a smaller lift on its own (flagged 55 % → 51 %, median macro error 0.40 → 0.36), but its effect on the worst cases is large: once water is represented, the dehydrated soup falls from a nine-fold to a 1.3-fold error and the split-pea soup from 2.2-fold to 1.3-fold. In raw mode the hardest groups remain Babyfoods, Soups, Sausages and Mixed Dishes, while Fast Foods and Baked Products are the most reliable.

One honest caveat bounds these numbers. The benchmark feeds exact CNF dish descriptions, which therefore self-match, so the 97 % catalog-hit and 99 % pass rate are an *upper bound* on real-world performance. A user who types a paraphrased or genuinely novel dish name will match the catalogue less often and fall through to the improved-but-imperfect decomposition, backstopped by the reconstruction gate. The force-decompose column (51 % flagged) is consequently the more representative figure for novel free-text input, and it marks the residual work: prompting alone does not fully solve cooked-dish energy density, which is why the catalog-preference and reconstruction-gate policy carries the load wherever a measured composition exists.

## 5. Case Study: Canadian Meals and Counterfactual Diet Shifts *(§5.2 populated 2026-05-26; §5.1 full S4 and §5.3–§5.5 pending)*

### 5.1 Scenario S4 — 100 representative Canadian meals

*Interim S4-lite panel (SHIPPED 2026-05-26).* Pending RDC access, we ran a **25-day curated fallback panel** (methodology: §4.6; [`results/S4-lite/meals_panel.csv`](results/S4-lite/meals_panel.csv)) spanning Western processed, Mediterranean, vegan, WAFCT West African, DASH, CFG-aligned, and active-adult patterns. Each day is a fixed CNF `{FoodID, mass_g}` list scored across all platform indicators via the production API. Spearman matrices and §6.2 trade-off exemplars are populated from this run.

*Source (full S4).* 2015 Canadian Community Health Survey–Nutrition Public-Use Microdata File (Statistics Canada, 2017), the same dataset and exclusion logic used by Brassard et al. (2022b, Methods p. 583): nationally representative of Canadians aged ≥ 1 y in private dwellings in the 10 provinces (excludes Territories, Forces members, reserves, remote areas, institutions), data collected 1 Jan – 31 Dec 2015, 24-hour recall via the Automated Multiple Pass Method, second recall obtained for ~37 % of respondents. We apply the same analytic exclusions as B7 (< 2 y; zero-energy reporters) for a national-comparable analytic frame of n = 20,103 respondents.

*Stratification and sampling.* 100 meal medoids drawn via k-medoids on nutrient/CNF-FoodID composition, stratified by meal occasion (breakfast/lunch/dinner/snack), three age-sex groups (2–18 y; males ≥ 19 y; females ≥ 19 y — matching the B7 stratification used to fit usual-intake models), and deprivation quintile.

*Usual-intake modelling.* For diet-level (i.FCS, HEFI-2019) reporting we apply the **NCI multivariate Markov-Chain Monte Carlo method** (Zhang et al., 2011) with 500 pseudo-individuals per respondent, episodically-consumed categories flagged at the ≥ 5 % zero-recall threshold (Krebs-Smith et al., 2010), and Balanced Repeated Replication on 500 Statistics Canada bootstrap weights — the exact machinery of Brassard et al. (2022b, Methods pp. 583–585).

*Benchmarks.* National HEFI-2019 distributions from Brassard et al. (2022b, Table A2 p. 591) are the comparison set. Reproducing the national mean of 43.1 / 80 (95 % CI 42.7–43.6) and the by-stratum means (2–18 y = 39.5; males ≥ 19 = 43.3; females ≥ 19 = 46.0) on our panel is the pre-registered sanity check before reporting any new finding. Indicators are reported as mean ± SD with pairwise Spearman correlation, a PCA biplot, and a Pareto frontier in (HENI gained, LCA single-score avoided) space.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 What the integrated pipeline delivers

The central result is not any single indicator — it is the **chain from intake to insight**. A user can photograph a granola bar at breakfast, type "jollof rice" at lunch, pick whole milk and oats directly from CNF search at dinner, review the aggregated day, save it, edit it later, and then see how that day scores on HEFI, HENI, HSR, Food Compass, environmental impact, dietary-pattern resemblance, and — critically — which ingredient swaps would move multiple metrics at once. Each step uses AI only where structured databases run out: label parsing, food matching, recipe decomposition, and FPED bridging. Everything downstream of those links stays deterministic, citation-ready, and reproducible.

That separation matters for *Nature Food* because the field has seen plenty of "AI nutrition" demos that collapse ingestion and scoring into one opaque model. We instead treat AI as plumbing — and the plumbing has to be testable. The FPED composition bridge (§3.6) is the clearest example: once HENI food-group masses come from the same USDA dataset Stylianou et al. used, cross-indicator rank coherence jumps from ρ = 0.77 to **ρ = 0.89** (HENI vs HEFI on the six-meal panel). The post-hoc label normaliser (§3.8.7) is the second: prompt engineering alone scored 0 % on dual-column panels; the normaliser recovered **88.2 %** field accuracy without touching the HSR math. SUBST-1 is the third: curated diet-shift rules replicate literature directions, but only after culinary and quality gates stop embedding retrieval from proposing egg→yolk or lean chicken→skin-on swaps on real recall days.

### 6.1b What AI adds — and what it doesn't

Constraining LLM calls to ambiguous foods (§3.4) and to retrieval-ranked candidates (§3.5) keeps the pipeline cheap, deterministic at the cache level, and auditable. We do not advocate end-to-end LLM scoring of nutrition or sustainability outcomes: epidemiological factors (HENI), LCA characterization (ReCiPe), and indicator algorithms (HEFI/HSR/FCS) are deterministic computations whose values must remain peer-reviewed and traceable. AI is bounded to classification and linkage — exactly the tasks where the literature shows LLMs are competitive with expert work.

### 6.2 Trade-offs across indicators

The S4-lite curated panel (§4.6; n = 25 full-day CNF diets; results at [`results/S4-lite/`](results/S4-lite/)) extends the §4.5 six-meal coherence check to five indicators including FCS and environmental global warming.

**Within nutrition, indicators agree.** Pairwise Spearman ρ across the 25 days: HEFI vs HENI **+0.837**, HEFI vs FCS **+0.797**, HSR vs FCS **+0.763**, HEFI vs HSR **+0.646** — extending the §4.5 six-meal coherence result (ρ = 0.886 HENI–HEFI) to a broader day-level panel. Low anchors (e.g. D01 deep-fried anti-pattern: HEFI 14/80, FCS 1) rank below high anchors (e.g. D10 CFG healthy plate: HEFI 62/80, FCS 100) on every nutrition metric.

**Nutrition and environment diverge — weakly.** Global-warming intensity correlates negatively with nutrition scores: HEFI vs GW **ρ = −0.42**, HENI vs GW **ρ = −0.59**, FCS vs GW **ρ = −0.51**. The sign matches Stylianou et al. (2021) and Cardinaals et al. (2024): better diet-quality scores tend toward lower per-kcal footprints, but the correlation is modest — plenty of room for trade-offs.

### 6.3 Decision-support and policy implications

Demand-side measures in IPCC AR6 require operational tooling at the granularity of meals and product categories. Open multi-indicator platforms reduce the cost of policy modelling, EAT–Lancet implementation studies, and dietary guideline revision.

### 6.4 Methodological contributions for food-systems AI

We argue that AI in food decision support is best deployed as: (i) a *linker* between heterogeneous data sources, (ii) a *fallback* when rule coverage is incomplete, and (iii) an *explainability layer* for user-facing copy grounded in source-paper caveats. PKG-IMG-1 adds a fourth bounded role: (iv) a *structured-ingestion bridge* from unstructured label images to deterministic scorers — with ingestion (multimodal LLM + post-hoc normaliser) separated from scoring (HSR rust_core; HEFI/HENI/FCS on CNF FoodIDs). SUBST-1 adds a fifth: (v) a *counterfactual reformulation layer* that applies explicit ingredient swaps and re-scores through the existing stack — now guarded by culinary plausibility filters, discovery quality gates, and FPED-aware ranking so suggestions reflect plate-level gaps, not just nutrient similarity (Cardinaals et al., 2024). A complementary choice — keeping nutrition out of the LCA functional unit and routing health effects through HENI — follows Weidema and Stylianou (2020): nutrient profiling and disease burden measure different things and should not be collapsed into one headline number.

## 7. Limitations

### 7.1 LCIA method limits inherited from ReCiPe 2016 v1.1

We inherit the documented gaps of ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 (Huijbregts et al., 2017, §4, pp. 144–145; RIVM 2017, §1.3, p. 20). The most consequential for food LCA:

- **No scenario differentiation** (I/H/E) for five categories — photochemical ozone formation, terrestrial acidification, freshwater eutrophication, land use, fossil resource scarcity — because value-choice data are not available in the underlying models.
- **Fossil resource scarcity** has no constant midpoint-to-endpoint factor; the endpoint must be resolved per resource (crude oil, hard coal, natural gas) and our pipeline treats this category as an exception (RIVM 2017, p. 25, Table 1.5, footnote 3).
- **Toxicity factors with restricted scope.** Cancer and non-cancer effects are linearised; agricultural and urban soil pathways are excluded to avoid double-counting with land use (Table 1.1, p. 18). Pesticide-residue ingestion is not modelled (Dekker et al., 2019, §4.2, p. 8 — and confirmed in AGRIBALYSE; ADEME, 2024).
- **Land use** is local-impact only; global extinction risk is not captured (Huijbregts et al., 2017, p. 145).
- **Egalitarian climate factors omit climate-carbon feedbacks** for non-CO₂ GHGs (RIVM 2017, footnote 1, p. 28).
- **Missing pathways**: indoor exposure, marine debris/invasive species, noise, nanoparticles, infectious-disease impacts of climate change (Huijbregts et al., 2017, §4.4, p. 145).

We explicitly flag the toxicity numbers in our outputs as low-confidence per the source documentation.

### 7.2 Reference-database limits

- AGRIBALYSE 3.2 carries known errata on egg-containing products, Bleu-Blanc-Coeur labelled products, quinoa, and seven Ciqual codes (ADEME, 2024, *Evolution* page); our pipeline either skips affected codes or applies the corrected values, with a complete log in the SI.
- AGRIBALYSE publishes only qualitative DQR (1–5); 67 % of products are at DQR ≤ 3 (ADEME, 2024). Our S3 uncertainty quantification therefore comes from Poore & Nemecek's deposited distributions rather than AGRIBALYSE itself.
- Using disaggregated AGRIBALYSE LCIs (vs. its open aggregated factor tables) requires an ecoinvent licence (ADEME, 2024, *Liens avec ecoinvent* page). Our open-source release uses the open factor tables; the licensed runs are noted in §3.5.

### 7.3 Nutritional-indicator limits inherited from the source instruments

- **HEFI-2019 has no absolute "aligned-diet" threshold** (Brassard et al., 2022a, Discussion p. 603; Brassard et al., 2022b, Discussion p. 588). The 99th percentile of Canadians ≥ 2 y is 62.9 / 80; full adherence is rare. We report HEFI-2019 only as relative differences across our diet scenarios — never as a pass/fail nutritional verdict.
- **HEFI-2019 component-level floor/ceiling effects** compress dynamic range on Protein foods (ceiling), Saturated fats, and Free sugars (Brassard et al., 2022a, Results p. 601; Brassard et al., 2022b, Discussion p. 588). Diet-shift signals will concentrate in V&F, Plant-based protein, Whole-grain foods, Grain-ratio and Beverages.
- **HEFI-2019 is NOT validated against health outcomes.** Brassard et al. (2022b, Discussion p. 589) state explicitly that the link between HEFI-2019 and disease endpoints is undetermined. HEFI-2019 is therefore reported as a *guideline-adherence* index, distinct from the disease-burden HENI score (which uses GBD epidemiology) and from the i.FCS score (which is mortality-validated).
- **Single one-day HEFI-2019 scores are not interpretable** as usual adherence (Brassard et al., 2022b, Discussion p. 588) — only the usual-intake NCI machinery (§5.1) supports inference at the meal-occasion level.
- **HEFI-2019 inherits the random and systematic error of 24-h recall data** (Brassard et al., 2022b, Discussion pp. 588–589); its validity for pregnant women, special-diet populations and clinical settings is undemonstrated.
- **HEFI-2019 C9 (free sugars) is currently computed from CNF total sugars** because the Rana et al. (2021) free-sugars supplement to CNF is not yet integrated. The API surfaces an explicit `c9_imputation_note` flagging that the total-sugar proxy systematically over-counts free sugars for foods with significant intrinsic sugars (whole fruit, milk, plain yogurt); supplement integration is logged as a data-dependent follow-up.
- **Cronbach's α = 0.66** (95 % CI 0.63–0.69; Brassard et al., 2022b, Results p. 587) — comparable to HEI-2015 α = 0.67 but below the conventional 0.70 reliability threshold. Authors attribute this to multidimensionality (≥ 4 PCA dimensions); we follow their convention of reporting total *and* component scores together.
- **HSR is a moving algorithm.** The point tables and star cut-offs are version-dependent and recommendations to better align with the Australian Dietary Guidelines will change product HSRs over time (Shahid et al., 2020, §4 p. 10). Our implementation pins **HSRC v9** (HSRAC, 10 December 2025, *Health Star Rating System Implementation Guide*, Appendix 1 Tables 1–7), which per its Appendix 5 changelog is functionally identical to v6/v7/v8 (no policy changes since v6); the cumulative differences from v5 (current at the time of the B13 uptake study) are limited to the Cat 1 energy rows 0–1 cap introduced in v4 (29 June 2021), which caps diet soft drinks at 3.5 stars, and the sweet-corn FVNL eligibility update introduced in v8 (21 September 2023).
- **HSR v9 implementation reconciliation.** Our Rust core was rewritten cell-by-cell against v9 Appendix 1 Tables 1–7, including the strict `>` threshold-evaluation convention ("≤X earns 0 points, >X earns 1 point"), the protein-eligibility rule on v9 page 26 (P points zeroed when baseline ≥ 13 and FVNL points < 5), and the v9 Table 7 star-cut-off arrays that admit negative final scores (Cat 2 maps ≤ −11 → 5.0 stars). The 10-canonical-AU-reference-food smoke test now reproduces all 10 foods within ±0.5 stars of the algorithm's correct v9 output. The Cat 1 "Water" → 5.0 stars and "Unsweetened Flavoured water" → 4.5 stars name-based overrides (v9 Table 7), the Cat 2 "Eligible fruits and vegetables" → 5.0 stars name-based override, the two-column FVNL handling for concentrated vs non-concentrated juices, and the sweet-corn FVNL eligibility classifier are logged as HSR-CODE-1.x follow-ups; none affect the numeric core or our diet-scenario results.
- **HSR FVNL inputs are commonly missing** on real packages (Shahid et al., 2020, §2.3 p. 3); our pipeline imputes them either from ingredient-list category-analogy (a precedent the HSR authors themselves use across ~700 subcategories) or via the FCS-10 ingredient-order weighting. This is a documented but unavoidable source of HSR variability.
- **Food Compass / i.FCS is built and validated on US data only** (NHANES/FNDDS/FPED), with thresholds anchored to the 5th/95th percentile of NHANES 2015–16 (Mozaffarian et al., 2021, Table S3 footnote †; Barrett et al., 2024, Methods p. 914). Direct transfer to CNF requires explicit category re-mapping; in practice the original FCS used 47 of 54 attributes in FNDDS because 7 (iodine, trans fat, 5 artificial additives) were unscorable.
- **FCS-10 has not been validated against health outcomes directly** (Barrett et al., 2025, Discussion p. 16). Health-outcome validation flows from i.FCS (O'Hearn et al., 2022; full FCS, n = 47 999 NHANES adults, all-cause mortality HR 0.93 per 1 SD) and FCS-10 inherits it only by proxy via Spearman r = 0.93 against full FCS.
- **i.FCS energy-weighting under-weights low-calorie foods** like fruits and vegetables — a design trade-off to avoid the water-content bias of per-kg weighting (O'Hearn et al., 2022, Discussion pp. 9–10).
- **Conflict-of-interest disclosure.** Senior authors of the Food Compass series (Mozaffarian, Blumberg) report extensive food-industry advisory and equity relationships (Barrett et al., 2024, Competing interests; O'Hearn et al., 2022, Competing interests; Barrett et al., 2025, Competing interests). We disclose this because Food Compass is part of our framework. Tufts is also considering commercial licensing of the full FCS implementation code, which is why our pipeline uses the openly-published FCS-10 (Barrett et al., 2025) and not full-FCS code.

### 7.4 HENI-specific limits inherited from the source instrument

- **HENI is a marginal index.** It estimates the health effect of adding or removing a single reference serving from the current diet, under additive-independence of risks, and is "not applicable to substantial changes in diet" (Stylianou et al., 2021, Discussion pp. 622–624; reaffirmed in Cardinaals et al., 2024, Discussion 4.4–4.7; Weidema & Stylianou, 2020, Results 3.2). Using marginal HENI for radically restructured diets risks over-estimating the gain from "healthy" diets and under-estimating the harm from "unhealthy" diets because real diet-level effects are multiplicative, not additive (Weidema & Stylianou, 2020, p. 1213). We therefore report HENI per dietary scenario as the *aggregated marginal effect of the substitutions* relative to baseline, and explicitly disclaim its use as a diet-level prediction.
- **HENI rests on observational diet-disease epidemiology of uneven evidence strength** (GBD 2017 Diet Collaborators, *Lancet* 2019;393:1958–1972, Discussion pp. 1968–1969). Effect sizes come mainly from meta-analyses of prospective cohort studies; residual confounding cannot be excluded, and "the strength of evidence is generally weaker than for established risks such as tobacco or systolic blood pressure, and varies across foods and nutrients" (p. 1969). Of the 15 dietary risks, 11 have a 94.9 % data-representativeness index, but processed meat, SSBs, trans fat (36.9 %) and sodium (26.2 %) are sparser (GBD 2017 Diet Collaborators, Table p. 1960).
- **Energy-adjustment / substitution ambiguity.** Most source cohorts adjust for total energy intake, so dietary risks are defined as shares of diet, not absolute exposure; the meta-analytic RRs therefore implicitly carry an unspecified substitution (GBD 2017 Diet Collaborators, Discussion p. 1968). This directly conditions our S5 counterfactuals (beef → legumes, etc.): the RR of a swap depends on what is substituted, and HENI's marginal logic is the food-item projection of this caveat. We report each diet shift with its explicit substitute and do not extrapolate to unspecified swaps.
- **Multiplicative-mediation assumption is "particularly relevant to analyses of dietary risk factors that yield protective effects, such as fruit or wholegrain intake"** (GBD 2023 Disease and Injury and Risk Factor Collaborators, *Lancet* 2025;406:1873–1922, Limitations p. 1902). Real combinations of dietary risks may be super- or sub-multiplicative; HENI inherits the GBD assumption.
- **Risk list is bounded by GBD scope.** HENI's 16 components correspond to 15 GBD dietary risks; **components not in the GBD are health-neutral by construction** (Stylianou et al., 2021, p. 624). This excludes added sugar (in base HENI; sensitivity in SI §S2.6), saturated fat (mediated through total cholesterol; SI §S2.7), vitamin D and shortfall nutrients, ultraprocessing, cooking effects, and bioavailability. Future GBD vintages may add or modify risks.
- **GBD vintage and the trans-fat TMREL.** Our HENI factors are taken from Stylianou et al. (2021) Suppl. Table 3 p. 8, which uses **GBD 2016** RRs. GBD 2023 confirms the 15-factor scaffold is unchanged but revises the TMREL for diet high in trans fatty acids (GBD 2023 Collaborators, p. 1880); a HENI recompute on GBD 2019 (the Cardinaals et al., 2024 vintage) or GBD 2023 is logged as future work. The trans-fat component itself is the weakest-supported in HENI: ~60–63 % of WWEIA foods have imputed TFA (regression R² = 0.69; Stylianou et al., 2021 SI §S2.1 p. 12) and the burden has been declining since the 2013 US partial-hydrogenation phase-out (SI §S2.8 pp. 29–31).
- **HENI uses an IMPACT World+ environmental track in the original paper** (Stylianou et al., 2021, Methods pp. 625–626; Bulle et al., 2019), whereas our pipeline applies ReCiPe 2016 v1.1 (§3.2). The HENI + ReCiPe combination is legitimate and follows the published precedent of Cardinaals et al. (2024, Methods 2.3), but endpoint factors and recommendation-zone thresholds taken from the HENI SI cannot be transplanted into a ReCiPe pipeline without re-derivation.
- **US-anchored RRs and burden rates.** Stylianou et al. (2021) compute DRFs from US-adult (NHANES) intakes and US-specific burden rates. Cardinaals et al. (2024) demonstrate portability by substituting GBD 2019 RRs and Dutch burden rates while preserving the algorithm; this is the procedure §3.9 inherits for Canada and is logged as future work pending a HENI-on-Canadian-burden refactor.
- **Distinction from DANI.** A sibling index, DANI (Weidema & Stylianou, 2020), uses **16 = 15 GBD risks + saturated fat** in μDALY per serving, while HENI uses **16 = 15 GBD risks + fibre split** in minutes of healthy life per serving (= −0.53 min × μDALY). We implement HENI, not DANI; the two share the same ~6,195-pair US factor-development effort but are *not* interchangeable, and our manuscript should never report a number under one label that was computed under the other.
- **Implementation reconciliation.** Our HENI factor table is pinned cell-by-cell to Stylianou et al. (2021) Supplementary Table 3 p. 8 in the published sign convention (negative DRF = beneficial), with the −0.5256 min/μDALY conversion (SI p. 98), TMREL hard caps (SI Table 1 pp. 4–5), and the milk-vs-calcium and fibre source-split carve-outs (SI §S2.9 pp. 35–36) implemented per the canonical methodology. The Stylianou SI §S2.2 (p. 13) chicken-wing worked example reproduces at −3.257 min/serving against the published −3.3 (Δ = +0.04 min). v1 simplifications relative to the full Stylianou framework are explicit and logged in [`code_action_items.md`](code_action_items.md) HENI-CODE-1.x: equal-share-per-outcome disease-attribution weights derived from SI Table 1 (rather than from the full 6,195-pair GBD 2016 RR matrix), zero-with-warning treatment of trans-fat when CNF lacks measured TFA (rather than the Stylianou imputation regression), and absolute-gram TMRELs for all components except PUFA and trans-fat (whose 11 %-energy and 0.5 %-energy caps surface as advisory warnings without modifying the contribution).

### 7.5 Benchmark and cohort limits

- The S1 benchmark cohort is drawn from CNF; transferability to FNDDS / FoodData Central foods requires re-labelling.
- In the open release, LCA factors remain group-level for 16 of the 18 standard ReCiPe 2016 midpoint categories — corresponding to the 14 EF-incompatible columns of §3.2 — **even on a high-confidence Agribalyse match**, because the open Agribalyse 3.2 *Tableur* catalogue carries no ReCiPe value to overlay for those categories (`Global warming` and `Stratospheric ozone depletion` are the two that *are* matcher-moved per matched row). This is a catalogue-content limit, not a matcher-confidence limit: item-level refinement on the other 16 categories awaits either ecoinvent-licensed LCI re-scoring under ReCiPe characterisation factors or a published ReCiPe-equivalent counterpart to the *Tableur*. The legacy below-threshold fallback to `cnf_integrator` group means (§3.5) compounds this on the long tail of unmatched CNF foods.
- **v1 scope trim — three categories, with uncertainty bands, not eighteen point estimates.** In the open release the pipeline aggregates exactly three ReCiPe 2016 H midpoint categories — `Global warming`, `Land use`, `Water consumption` — and reports each as a low / central / high envelope rather than a single point. The central values are derived deterministically (§3.7 "Per-food-group factor derivation") from Poore & Nemecek (2018, *Science* 360:987–992) Fig. 1 panel means via explicit protein-content (Panel A) / density (Panel B) / kcal-density (Panel C) / per-kg (Panels E–F) conversion constants, and from Mekonnen & Hoekstra (2011, *Hydrol Earth Syst Sci* 15:1577, crops Table 3; 2012, *Ecosystems* 15:401, animals Table 3) **blue-water-only consumptive** footprints for Water consumption — not the green+blue+grey total footprint, the use of which would inflate ReCiPe Water Consumption Potential by 10–30× and was the dominant value-correctness defect we found in internal audit (`backend/_smoke_validate_cnf_integrator.py`). The derivation chain is now module-scope (`_PN_PANEL_CENTRALS` + `_DERIVATION_CONSTANTS` + `_derive_group_centrals()` in `cnf_integrator.py`) and gated by an independent panel-anchored validator that recomputes each group's central from the raw panel values plus the conversion constants — replacing an earlier hand-typed table whose Beef Products GHG silently averaged beef-herd and dairy-herd values (now consistently beef-herd only at 10.0 kg CO₂-eq / 100 g), whose Dairy and Egg Products Land used cheese only at 9.0 m²·yr / 100 g (now an arithmetic blend of cheese + milk + egg components at 3.52, removing a 2.5× over-statement of dairy/egg land impact), and whose Cereals, Grains and Pasta values applied a 350 kcal / 100 g dry-grain assumption to as-consumed CNF entries (now 200 kcal / 100 g, yielding GHG 0.18 and Land 0.28 vs 0.32 / 0.49 previously). The low-bound envelope uses Poore & Nemecek 10th-percentile/mean ratios per food panel; the high-bound uses a conservative ~2–3× mean proxy reflecting P&N's documented within-product log-skew (full per-product PDFs are in their Data S1). Bands are meal-level worst/best-case envelopes, not a 90 % CI; Monte-Carlo propagation over per-food PDFs is deferred to v2 (see code_action_items TODO-CODE-LCA-3). Matcher-supplied per-food values inherit the same group-level uncertainty ratios rather than a flat ±1.5× proxy, so the envelope width reflects within-product spread regardless of whether the value came from an Agribalyse match or the group-default fallback. **Independent validation against Stylianou et al. (2021, *Nat Food* 2:616–627) IMPACT World+ per-serving means for five anchor foods (beef, processed cheddar, chicken broiler, fluid 2 % milk, raw apple) confirms that the worst/best-case envelopes empirically bracket the published literature values in all five cases, even though the central estimates sit 0.49–1.18× of the Stylianou centrals — the bands are wide enough to honestly contain the documented P&N-vs-IMPACT-World+ inter-method spread.** The bracketing test harness is at [`backend/_smoke_api_vs_literature.py`](backend/_smoke_api_vs_literature.py) and runs against the live API. The remaining fifteen ReCiPe categories (terrestrial acidification, freshwater + marine + terrestrial eutrophications [unit-incompatible with P&N's PEF-aggregate kg-SO₂-eq and kg-PO₄-eq indicators], plus the twelve toxicity / ecotoxicity / ozone-formation / radiation / PM / resource-scarcity midpoints that have no per-food-group numerical literature target on any basis we could retrieve) are **not** in the v1 consumed midpoint vector. Shipping them as point estimates with no defensible source presented false multidimensional rigor; honest narrow coverage beats invented breadth. They re-enter the consumed set once the licensed AGRIBALYSE-LCI-re-scored-under-ReCiPe work lands (code_action_items TODO-CODE-LCA-2). The Resources endpoint is correspondingly reported as `None` rather than `0` so it cannot silently bias the single score downward. The two anchored climate-and-land categories sit roughly 2–4× above Stylianou et al. (2021) IMPACT World+ per-serving averages for the same food groups, reflecting differences in characterisation method (P&N meta-analysis vs IMPACT World+), inventory database (mixed vs WFLDB), and producer-tail inclusion; readers should treat the cnf_integrator means as conservative P&N-anchored point estimates within a documented inter-method spread rather than as the single defensible value.
- **Documented derivation-constant approximations.** The transparent derivation chain (§3.7) makes the underlying assumptions explicit and therefore separately debatable: (i) protein fractions are group typical values (beef / pork / nuts = 0.20; poultry / cheese = 0.22; finfish = 0.18; eggs = 0.12; cooked legumes = 0.09; **dry beans would require 0.22, not 0.09**, so the legume value is biased toward as-consumed cooked entries and would under-state impact for dry-bean preparations); (ii) the cereal kcal density of 200 / 100 g is a representative mid for CNF as-consumed cereal entries but individual foods within the group span 130–350 kcal / 100 g (a per-food kcal-density lookup against `cnf_integrator.get_nutrient_amount(food_id, 'ENERGY (KILOCALORIES)')` is a defensible v2 refinement); (iii) the Dairy and Egg Products blend is arithmetic-mean across cheese, milk and egg components rather than consumption-weighted (a per-CNF-entry dietary-frequency weight would require Canadian intake-survey microdata at a finer resolution than the food-group bucket); (iv) the Beef Products derivation uses beef-herd only on the basis that CNF "Beef Products" excludes dairy-herd by-product meat as a separate category. Each of these is now visible in `_DERIVATION_CONSTANTS` for reviewer audit; the prior hand-typed centrals masked these choices behind opaque numbers.
- **Group-mean is a known structural limitation, not the target architecture.** The CNF `FoodGroupName` buckets are nutritional taxonomies, not environmental ones — "Dairy and Egg Products" contains skim milk and aged cheddar (≈10× apart on GWP); "Vegetables and Vegetable Products" contains raw cucumber and potato crisps (similarly wide once processing is counted). Within-group variance dominates between-group variance for food LCA (Poore & Nemecek 2018, p. 2; Fig. 3), so a single group vector throws away most of the real signal even when the value is well-grounded. The v1 release routes through group means for foods where the §3.5 matcher cannot resolve a per-food Agribalyse row, but the per-category audit-trail now explicitly tags fallback values as `fallback_low_confidence:group_default` so consumers can see what is a per-food measurement versus a group-mean extrapolation. **As of the 2026-05-22 architecture work, three complementary mitigations have landed in the open release** (code_action_items TODO-CODE-LCA-3): (i) `Tier α` — multi-basis functional-unit output exposes `per_serving`, `per_100g_product`, `per_100_kcal`, and `per_100g_protein` simultaneously, addressing the per-100-g-product critique by letting the consumer choose; (ii) `Tier β` — matcher retrieval improvements (food-state canonicalisation that strips raw/cooked/frozen/canned modifiers before retrieval, subgroup routing for composite-y CNF groups like Babyfoods → `aliments infantiles`) lift retrieval quality on the long tail of CNF foods that no Agribalyse v32 entry mirrors textually; (iii) `Tier γ` — `RecipeDecomposer` (default off via `enable_recipe_decomposer`) is a new composite-food fallback that asks an LLM to express a CNF dish as a mass-weighted ingredient list constrained to retrieved Agribalyse v32 entries (rejecting hallucinated Ciqual codes at parse time, validating against mass conservation ± 5 g and an unresolved-mass cap of 10 %, requiring self-reported decomposition confidence ≥ 0.6), then mass-weights each ingredient's per-100 g impact to produce a per-food impact dict that bypasses the group mean entirely for the ~8 CNF FoodGroups dominated by composites (Mixed Dishes, Soups, Fast Foods, Babyfoods, Sausages, Sweets, Snacks, Baked Products). The remaining v2 work — full commodity-level LCI re-scoring under licensed AGRIBALYSE-LCI — is tracked as TODO-CODE-LCA-2 and is blocked on ecoinvent licensing.
- The 2015 CCHS–Nutrition is the most recent open Canadian 24-hour recall dataset; results are presentational, not forecasts. The B7 validation sample (n = 20 103) excludes Canadians on full-time military service, residents of the Territories, on-reserve Indigenous populations, residents of remote areas, and institutionalised individuals (Brassard et al., 2022b, Methods p. 583) — a limit to external validity our manuscript must repeat verbatim.
- **Monte Carlo iteration count vs input-distribution sample size.** §3.9 runs N = 10 000 iterations per meal. Heijungs (2020, *Int J Life Cycle Assess.* 25:394–402, p. 401) demonstrates that running N_run ≫ n (where n is the per-input sample size used to estimate each input distribution) shrinks the output CI beyond what the input data warrant, converting imprecision into invisible inaccuracy. Poore & Nemecek (2018) producer sample sizes vary 30–2 800 per food group; for the sparsest groups our N_run exceeds the recommended cap. A per-group sensitivity check at N_run = min(n_i, 500) for the ten sparsest groups is logged as SI work. The pedigree-matrix fallback path of §3.9 ((c)) is itself flagged by Heijungs (p. 401) as "incompatible with large-scale Monte Carlo simulations" — corroborated independently by Kim et al. (2025, *J Ind Ecol.* 29:1090–1104, p. 1101) for ecoinvent pedigree-derived biosphere distributions — and we treat its use as transparency-only metadata rather than a defensible CI source.
- **Sobol independence assumption (Kim et al. 2025 caveat).** The first/total-order Sobol indices in §3.9 assume input-parameter independence. Kim et al. (2025, §1; Fig. 1 basmati-rice example) show that independent sampling of correlated technosphere inputs (market shares, ENTSO-E electricity time series, carbon-balanced inventories) systematically inflates apparent uncertainty and produces false-positive sensitivity rankings. Our v1 pipeline does not enforce correlation across inputs; the SHAP/XGBoost-based correlated-sampling extension Kim et al. document (§§2.2, 2.4) is logged as a v2 upgrade path. v1 Sobol attributions should therefore be interpreted as upper bounds on per-factor variance contribution.
- **Cohort-geography skew in dietary-risk evidence (EAT–Lancet acknowledgement).** Our HENI factors (Stylianou et al., 2021) inherit from GBD diet-risk meta-analyses whose primary source cohorts are concentrated in high-income settings; the EAT–Lancet Commissions explicitly flag the same gap (Rockström et al., 2025, Panel 1; Willett et al., 2019, §1, pp. 456–457) — LIC/MIC cohort coverage for meat-, dairy-, and processed-food-health associations is sparse. Our manuscript inherits this skew unchanged.

### 7.6 AI-system limits

- Inference cost will change as model prices change; we report tokens to allow extrapolation.
- LLM outputs are non-deterministic above zero temperature; we hold temperature at 0 for the categorizer and matcher and cache outputs by SHA-256 of the prompt to make runs reproducible. **Caveat:** Ase et al. (2026) report their 90.3–94.2 % expert-agreement numbers at temperature 1.0 per Gemini's documented default, so their published figures are not a like-for-like benchmark for our deterministic categorizer.
- Provider-specific behaviour: we run the same prompts against `gpt-4o-mini`, `claude-haiku-4-5` and one Gemini variant (S1 ablation) so that no single vendor's idiosyncrasies dominate results. Ase et al.'s "dominant" (multi-model consensus) protocol matched or slightly beat the best single model on every metric; we report inter-provider κ on the S1 sample as a robustness check.
- **Matcher accuracy is hostage to retrieval quality.** Zhou et al. (2025) NutriRAG report that RAG performance degrades when the relevant external data are limited or biased; our §3.5 matcher inherits this caveat — its accuracy is bounded by the coverage and representativeness of the Agribalyse candidate pool and the embedding index. Concretely, the bootstrap catalog covers ~50–80 Agribalyse entries across the 14 Poore & Nemecek food groups; the path to the full 2,518-entry Agribalyse 3.2 ingest is documented in `code_action_items.md` GROUP-D-CODE-1.x-A.
- **Prompting-only ceiling on food → ontology linking.** Gjorgjevikj et al. (2026, FoodyLLM) demonstrate that 5-shot prompting of general-purpose LLMs reaches only macro F1 = 0.33–0.51 on food → ontology linking; only LoRA fine-tuning lifts this to 0.86–0.97. We do not fine-tune (§3.4), so the §3.5 matcher's S7 accuracy expectation is anchored on the RAG-prompting upper end (~0.82 micro F1 on the closest analogous 51-class NER task; Zhou et al., 2025) rather than the fine-tuned ceiling.
- **Composite-food / meal-level gap.** Furrer et al. (2024) excluded composite foods (≈22 % of EuroFIR; ≈2 % of Agribalyse) from their EuroFIR ↔ Agribalyse interlinkage case study because recipe-formulation gaps make descriptor matching unreliable. Our pipeline attempts composite-food matching via per-ingredient decomposition (CNF carries the ingredient list) followed by per-ingredient embedding retrieval and weight-aggregated matching, but the recipe-composition gap is real; per-food matcher confidence on composites is reported separately from single-food confidence in §4.4 / Scenario S7.
- **Multimodal LLM structural drift varies by input type (PKG-IMG-1 finding).** The four-run A/B benchmark in §3.8.7.1 demonstrated that targeted prompt-level fixes (Sahoo et al., 2024 arXiv:2402.07927v2 — few-shot example + schema-as-code + Chain-of-Verification) raise raw schema compliance from 0 % to 80 % and recover one of two previously-catastrophic image cases, but **still produce zero-accuracy structural failures on the FDA dual-column "per serving / per container" panel** (the single-column granola-bar worked example primes the LLM into picking the wrong column). The CoVe self-verification step does not detect these structural failures — the model "verifies" malformed output as correct. Belt-and-suspenders (enhanced prompt + normaliser, V4 in the benchmark) can be *worse* than either layer alone because the normaliser is tuned for the production prompt's output shape and can subtractively mangle structurally-correct enhanced-prompt outputs. We therefore ship a post-hoc-normaliser architecture paired with the production prompt's lighter critical-fields-list approach rather than a heavier-prompt architecture. New LLM output-shape variants are addressed by extending the alias dictionary (`_NUMERIC_SUBKEY_ALIASES` and `_FLAT_NUTRIENT_ALIASES` in [`packaged_food_extractor.py`](backend/api/services/packaged_food_extractor.py); ~10 LoC per variant, validated via the existing 60-call A/B harness) rather than re-engineering the prompt.
- **Vision-model accuracy ceilings on small / typographically-compact label text.** Two specific failures are documented in §3.8.7.1's audit and persist on gpt-4o-mini across all four pipeline variants tested: (a) gpt-4o-mini cannot reliably read "8 g" vs "3 g" on small bilingual fibre rows (the Sea Family Foods organic-soup tub label, validated as 8 g via the 29 % DV cross-check against Health Canada's 28 g reference), and (b) the Canadian "Saturated / saturés 0.4 g + Trans / trans 0 g" combined-row typographic convention is read as saturated-only with trans returned null. Neither is a prompt or schema issue — they are model accuracy limits on visual digit-discrimination + compact-row parsing. **Opus 4.7 resolves the infant-formula regime entirely** (8/8 ground-truth fields on Gerber Good Start Plus 1 RTF per §3.8.7.2) but at ~10× the per-extraction cost; the platform exposes this as an opt-in provider override rather than a default. Quality on compact bilingual rows remains bounded above by the chosen multimodal model; the user-facing prefill form is designed to surface low-confidence fields for manual correction before scoring.
- **Packaged-food ingredient composition is INFERRED, not measured (PKG-IMG-1).** Regulation only requires ingredient lists to be in descending-mass-order; percentages are rarely disclosed (EU Regulation 1169/2011 QUID rules being the main exception). The Stage-2 decomposer therefore produces an *inferred* composition constrained by descending order + NF panel macro reconciliation (± 10 % per macro) + mass conservation (± 5 % of net weight) + a constrained CNF-candidate-pool — not a measurement. Downstream caveats surface at recall review (`packaged_food_inferred_at_N_occasion(s)` warnings), scan confirmation, list-level amber banners on the Scorecard, and backend `inferred_composition_caveat` merge on all six scorer endpoints when `decomposition_provenance='packaged_food_inferred'`. The 5-image test panel does not capture every label format encountered in production; infant-formula RTF labels additionally require a user-supplied net-weight anchor when the panel reports mL only (§3.8.7.2 finding 4).
- **Recipe-decomposition accuracy on novel dishes is bounded, and the headline figure is an upper bound (DECOMP-VALID).** The reconstruction benchmark of §3.8.9–§4.7 feeds exact CNF dish descriptions, which therefore self-match, so the 97 % catalog-hit and 99 % production pass rate overstate performance on free-text input. A user who types a paraphrased or genuinely novel dish name matches the catalogue less often and falls through to the raw decomposition, which still flags about half of composites for nutrient error (51 % in force-decompose mode) even after the cooked-form prompt rule; the nutrient-reconstruction gate backstops these only when a confident catalogued match exists. Cooked, diluted, and processed composites — soups, baby foods, sausages, mixed dishes — remain the hardest cases, and prompting alone does not fully resolve their energy density. We report the force-decompose figures alongside the production figures precisely so the residual risk is visible rather than hidden behind the catalog shortcut.
- **Substitution suggestions inherit upstream composition uncertainty (SUBST-1).** Swaps applied to PKG-IMG-1–decomposed packaged products or LLM-decomposed homemade dishes propagate inference error into counterfactual deltas. HENI swap aggregates are **marginal food-item effects** and are not valid for large multi-ingredient reformulations without caution (Stylianou et al., 2021, Discussion pp. 622–624). Automated suggestions pass through culinary guards, discovery quality gates, and FPED-aware ranking ([`substitution_culinary.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_culinary.py), [`substitution_quality.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_quality.py), [`substitution_fped_ranking.py`](backend/api/services/substitution_fped_ranking.py)) but remain modelling aids; regional and homemade dishes may require manual ingredient selection. Each reported swap names an explicit replacement food (GBD 2017 energy-substitution caveat; §7.4). Isolated-portion swaps (e.g. milk→soy on a single beverage serving) can show HEFI regressions even when whole-day context would favour the swap — users should interpret suggestions in the context of the full day composition.

## 8. Conclusion

Diet quality, disease burden, nutrient profiling, and environmental impact each tell part of the story — but until now, scoring all of them from the same real eating record required expert hours, manual database lookups, and a tolerance for single-metric simplification. **ecodish365** shows that an open, auditable pipeline can do better: five peer-reviewed indicators on one CNF-anchored food catalogue, with AI confined to linking tasks (food matching, label parsing, recipe decomposition, FPED bridging) and every score traceable to published factors.

Three results anchor the claim. First, once HENI's food-group masses come from FPED — the same dataset behind the original HENI paper — independently designed nutrition indicators rank meals in strong agreement (ρ = 0.89 HENI–HEFI on a six-meal panel; ρ = 0.84 on 25 curated days). Second, multimodal label extraction works in production only when a post-hoc normaliser sits between the LLM and the schema validator (88.2 % field accuracy vs 0 % without it on dual-column panels). Third, literature-anchored diet shifts replicate at the ingredient level (4/4 directional checks) and, when overlaid onto Western processed days, move lose–lose patterns toward win–win on health and footprint — but only when substitution suggestions pass culinary and quality gates that stop embedding retrieval from surfacing anatomically implausible swaps.

The platform is released under Apache 2.0 with smoke-test harnesses for every AI subsystem, open prompts, and workbook-checksummed LCA factors. Full population-scale validation on 2015 CCHS-Nutrition medoids, expert-labelled LLM categorization (n = 500), and Monte Carlo uncertainty bands remain pending RDC access — but the interim panels already establish the multi-indicator trade-off structure this field needs: nutrition indicators agree with each other more than any of them agree with environmental intensity, so reporting all five — and showing users which swaps improve which — is not optional decoration. It is the point.
